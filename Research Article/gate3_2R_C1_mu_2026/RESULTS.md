# Mu C1 results

The cytokine replay shows a smaller initial-priming increment in T3 cultures in both later-priming strata. During aging, the lower blood myeloid fraction coincided with higher absolute lymphocyte counts and little change in absolute neutrophil or monocyte counts in a separate cohort. Liver histology and young-recipient experiments separate macrophage abundance from the inflammatory fraction. The lineage-transfer endurance contrast favors the myeloid arm descriptively.

Each contrast compares the initially primed and unprimed groups while holding later myeloid treatment fixed. The values favor attenuation associated with initial priming in this experiment. ## S2. Myeloid supply and tissue inflammation

T3 minus Cre marrow MPP3 was −11.95 points. Blood myeloid fractions were similar or mixed through 12 months and lower across the four older age strata, reaching −18.01 points at 24–25 months. In the separate aged CBC cohort, absolute neutrophil and monocyte differences were −0.054 and +0.008 on the published scale, while the lymphocyte difference was +2.667. These comparisons support a denominator contribution and leave a generalized absolute myeloid deficit unresolved. Aged serum TNF and IL-6 differences were −14.88 and −105.69 pg/mL. In the aged marrow chimeras, liver CD68 abundance was −27.34 in the published scale and the IL-6-high fraction within CD68-positive cells was −19.76 percentage points. Both tissue readouts shifted, with possible macrophage-subset selection and unknown section nesting. No cross-panel regression or pairing was used. ## S3. Transfer, recipient amplification and physiology

Old-versus-young splenocyte transfer was associated with +7.85 pg/mL serum IL-6, +46.92 on the CD68 abundance scale and +18.16 percentage points in the high-IL-6 fraction. The old-minus-young time-to-exhaustion difference was −2.70 minutes before transfer and −20.70 minutes after. Inverted-screen means were close at both times. Anti-IL-1β versus IgG reduced CD68 abundance by 28.00 and the high-IL-6 fraction by 10.24 points, while endurance was 18.31 minutes higher. T3 rather than Cre splenocytes from separate aged chimeras yielded reductions of 20.28 and 15.21 points on the respective tissue measures and a 17.20-minute higher endurance mean. The blockade locates an IL-1β-sensitive step in the recipient experiment. Cell dose was fixed by design. Donor composition and post-transfer persistence are unresolved for the reported source values.

Fig. 7b group means were 29.33 and 55.91 minutes for Cre and T3 myeloid, and 53.31 and 54.63 minutes for Cre and T3 T-cell recipients. The component differences were +26.58 and +1.32 minutes, yielding a **+25.26-minute interaction**. The caption's 12/13/12/13 counts match the plotted order, Cre-myeloid, T3-myeloid, Cre-T, T3-T. The observation columns have a different order. Cached means agree to floating-point precision under the named mapping. Distance also favored the myeloid contrast (+361.53 versus +33.96 m), whereas inverted-screen latency increased in both lineages (+39.81 versus +23.71 s). The contrast estimates recipient outcomes without donor-cluster precision. Fig. 7 does not supply a paired macrophage IL-6 readout. The temporal and recipient links required to attribute endurance to the observed inflammatory changes remain unresolved. Glucose tolerance and alveolar panels remain published context. ## S4–S6. Upstream RNA and the persistence question

The four HSC count libraries reproduce the published direction for Cox7a2, Ndufa6 and the released labels ND1 and ND4. All four were higher in each T3 library than in either Cre library after median-ratio normalization. The T3-minus-Cre normalized count differences were +98.78, +146.32, +1,445.75 and +3,582.57, respectively. These are bulk library counts, distinct from respiration and from the recipient measurements. The RNA workbook provides no source-animal or pooling key. Ross et al. change the representation of myeloid-biased HSCs directly. Mu measures MPP3 frequency, mature blood fractions and selected absolute counts, and transfers enriched mature cells. Those endpoints keep precursor selection plausible without resolving it. Zeng et al. measure human HSC accessibility and expression after inflammatory recovery. Mu compares genotypes under ongoing SIRT3 expression, and the bulk counts lack a within-subset recovery contrast. This holds cross-study signature scoring under S5. A common exposure followed by recovery, matched rechallenge and tracked donor lineages would help distinguish a retained imprint from continuing expression.
