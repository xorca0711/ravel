# When does an inflammatory phenotype qualify as hematopoietic memory?

**Question for interpreting C1.** Reduced training-associated cytokines, a change in myeloid cell supply, persistence after a transient insult and a functional effect in a young recipient are four different observations. The same aging hematopoietic system can show several. The informative comparison depends on which transition the dataset actually preserves.

| Study and exact locator | What its design resolves | How it changes the Mu analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Brown et al. (2013), Figs. 2–4 | SIRT3 affects HSC self-renewal under aging or transplant stress. Old-cell overexpression, ROS and reconstitution were already studied. | Improvement in regeneration alone is an established premise. Mu’s increment concerns downstream inflammatory behavior and consequences for distant tissues. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.01.005) |
| Mu et al. (2026), Extended Data Fig. 1b–g and Figs. 2–7 | Priming and challenge separate stages of immune response. Old splenocyte transfer into a young host and IL-1β blockade place parts of the recipient cascade after transfer. Sorted myeloid versus T-cell transfers test lineage dependence of physiology. | Start the reproduction with priming-specific cytokine contrasts. Analyze blood composition and the inflammatory fraction within macrophages before interpreting endurance. The linked correction amends K. Claire Tsuei’s name. | Ross et al. (2024), Figs. 2–4 | Selective depletion of myeloid-biased HSCs in aged mice changes hematopoietic composition and selected immune functions. | A changed HSC mixture is an experimentally supported route to altered output. Evaluate it beside within-state evidence in Mu. Different interventions and downstream endpoints preclude attributing Mu’s result to the same route. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07238-x) |
| Zeng et al. (2026), Figs. 1–3 and 5 | Human HSC inflammation–recovery xenografts identify a subset retaining accessibility and expression differences after recovery. The paper also examines enrichment in human cohorts and transmission of a program to progeny. | This is a closer test of persistence after an initiating exposure than a genotype comparison under continuous SIRT3 expression. Use the actual recovery design and its subset-specific signatures to interpret what the mouse work can establish. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10522-7) |

**What can be compared after reproduction.** For transferability, compare the lineage and recipient endpoints within Mu. For molecular persistence, Zeng’s recovery comparison and Supplementary Tables 8–9/12 supply the external reference. If testing a signature, distinguish donor subset frequency from expression in comparable HSCs, map orthologues before scoring and identify which human data are newly generated or reused. A matched direction under different species, stimuli and timing addresses generalization. A common causal SIRT3 mechanism remains open. Historical X3 makes a comparison with epithelial inflammatory memory. Its remaining question concerns which observations show persistence after withdrawal in each tissue.
