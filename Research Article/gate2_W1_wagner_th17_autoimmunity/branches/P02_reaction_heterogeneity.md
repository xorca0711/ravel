# Wg-P02: opposing reactions within a pathway

**Question/decision:** Which source-defined reaction associations are obscured
when summarized as a pathway? Decide whether a proposed metabolic readout needs
reaction/compartment resolution. This is replication and measurement validation.

**Published starting point:** Wagner Fig. 2C-E reports heterogeneous reaction
behavior. Wang et al. 2025, Fig. 1-4, supplies a close PGAM precedent; this branch
cannot claim discovery of a uniformly pro-inflammatory glycolysis exception.
No local reaction contrasts have been computed.

**Hypothesis:** Declared pathway summaries can hide reproducible opposing
reaction directions. **Strongest rival:** reaction direction/compartment errors,
metareaction duplication or unstable weak signals generate apparent opposition.

**Tuple/unit/endpoint:** Source sorted Th17n/p, original collection; biological
preparation/animal for inferential uncertainty. Endpoint is a qualified reaction
or metareaction contrast and its discrepancy from a frozen pathway summary on
the same cells. Freeze Recon2 mappings, confidence/EC universe and group-level
testing; do not count multiple members of one metareaction as independent hits.

**Discriminator and action:** Persistent opposition under the declared source
and unit checks supports a reaction-resolved assay choice. Loss after correcting
mappings weakens the representation claim. Missing unit information allows only
source descriptive reconstruction. Report all prespecified central-carbon and
polyamine contrasts, including agreement and weak/discordant results.

**Limit/hold:** This is not proof of carbon routing or an inhibitor's target
specificity. A comparison with the later PGAM paper must audit reused 2015
inputs separately from its new experiments; paper count is not replication.
R1 mapping/concordance is required before extending the result.
