# P02 — reaction resolution required by the question

**Wg-P02 · Proposed measurement question · Outcome-exposed.**
[Prior branch](../branches/P02_reaction_heterogeneity.md) · [Derivation index](README.md).

## Observation → gap → question

R1 reports mixed source-significant directions in 20/53 display pathways.
Glycolysis instead has 19 positive and no negative source-significant groups
among 29. These observations remain cell-level descriptive source statistics.
The 1,912-versus-1,911 metareaction discrepancy prevents exact reproduction claims.
See [the result](../SOURCE_SCORE_RESULTS.md) and [Figure 3's reaction table](../FIGURES.md#figure-3-which-reaction-scores-differ).

**Question:** Which apparent pathway-level responses conceal directionally
different reaction associations that survive direction, compartment and GPR
qualification?

The biological purpose is to choose the resolution of a future measurement.
Finding such heterogeneity is not itself a new mechanism or enzyme nomination.

## Hypothesis and strongest rival

Some opposing associations persist under correctly specified source mappings,
so a pathway aggregate loses information relevant to the measured comparison.
The strongest rival is erroneous reaction direction/compartment assignment,
duplicate metareactions, weak signal or preparation effects.

## Unit, contrast and endpoint

Use the same Th17p/n context as [P01](P01.md), with a fixed reaction family and
pathway summary. Endpoint: reaction/metareaction contrast and its discrepancy
from that summary on the same observations. Cells and duplicated reaction
members are not independent biological replicates. Population uncertainty needs
the missing preparation identities; biochemical routing needs direct assays.

Keep enzyme abundance, GPR-defined reaction capacity, inferred score and measured
metabolic routing as distinct quantities. Shared enzymes cannot be ranked for
causal importance from their associated reaction-score q values.

## Informative outcomes

| Outcome | Interpretation and action |
|---|---|
| Opposition persists after prespecified mapping checks | Retain reaction/compartment resolution when choosing a biochemical readout |
| Opposition disappears after correcting mapping/grouping | Narrow the aggregation claim; preserve the original source reconstruction and issue a versioned correction if required |
| Aggregates and qualified reactions agree | The aggregate may suffice for this contrast; no need to force heterogeneity |
| Missing preparation map or unresolved weak signal | Descriptive annotation only; no animal-level enzyme selection |

## Prior work, contribution and readiness

Wagner already reports reaction heterogeneity; PGAM is a close experimental
glycolysis precedent. Their exact comparisons and the unresolved PGAM signature
orientation are documented in [the audit](../PRECEDENT_REVIEW.md). The possible
increment is measurement qualification, not discovery of a glycolytic exception.

This branch supports assay specification for later biological work; it is not
a separate global RQ merely because it produces more detailed plots. Mapping
qualification and a frozen exposed E3 contract are the next computational
requirements. A new favorable pathway cannot replace the prespecified family.
