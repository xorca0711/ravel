## Organizing question and what the completed work changed

**When does selective immune modulation restore neuronal production in an aging
neurogenic niche, and which part of the response limits sustained recovery?**

The owner's aging–damage–recovery interest gives this question its purpose.
Physiological aging, recovery of neuronal production and repair after a defined
lesion remain different comparisons. An aged-brain study need not first become
an injury study to be useful.

Navarro's T-cell/interferon explanation is already experimentally tested;
rediscovering that relationship from RNA would add no new mechanism. The
[current animal audit](RESULTS.md), [RNA interpretation](INTERPRETATION_20261108.md)
and [human-context follow-up](followup_20261110/README.md) instead narrow what
can be carried forward:

- Selected immune RNA responses separate WT and D25K, with exceptions and
  some markers remaining above old control. A uniformly restored niche is not
  established. Pooling prevents animal-level treatment-effect estimation.
- Neural-marker changes are measured, including EdU/NeuN, but direct D25K–WT
  superiority is not established. Direct cognition comparisons remain
  nonsignificant; this does not establish equivalence or no benefit.
- Human GZMB consistency and mixed IFNG directions concern a different agonist
  and peripheral cells. They support keeping outputs separate; they do not
  identify a brain mechanism or supply an age contrast.
- Statistical reconciliation and source identity work are complete within their
  holds. Repeating them would not decide where neuronal recovery is constrained.

Thus the next biological uncertainty concerns the recipient neural compartment
and the duration/stage of its output. Existing results do not establish that
this is the actual bottleneck; that is a proposed explanation to examine.

## Extension 1: distinguish recipient-state representation from response

**Question:** under the qualified D25K/WT alias pair, do neural/glial RNA
differences reflect changed representation of independently defined states,
responses within comparable states, or both?

Navarro Fig. 6B–D already reports pathway contrasts in broad neural/glial bins.
Repeating a broad IFN score alone would not advance this question. The proposed
increment is attribution of a qualified recipient pattern to representation
and within-state response, where it could change the population or endpoint
chosen for a later-output comparison. Check Fig. S6/Table S3 and annotation
support before freezing work; if they already answer the exact contrast, reuse
that evidence. Those supplementary products were not fully audited for this
new comparison in the present reading pass.

This is a bounded, outcome-exposed descriptive extension of the existing immune
screen. Use the qualified main pair and old control, with young control only as
an age reference. The unit is a condition library/pool; individual cells do not
provide animal replication. The endpoint is measured RNA/detection and observed
state representation. RNA cannot by itself identify IFN type, secretion,
phosphorylation, transitions, survival or functional rescue.

Before execution, qualify neural identities and a literature-defined marker
panel. The original Astrocytes_qNSCs and aNSCs_NPCs bins combine populations:
they cannot establish an NSC-specific result without further identity evidence.
Population redistribution and within-state response are both potentially useful
findings. Preserve both; neither is automatically nuisance. Marker overlap,
cell-cycle composition, sampling and annotation are consequential alternatives.
Do not derive states from the same outcome vector and then call their differences
independent validation. No automatic enrichment or trajectory queue follows.

| Possible result | What changes next |
|---|---|
| Immune-associated differences coexist with residual recipient IFN RNA | Nominate that compartment for a matched direct-response/neuronal-output comparison; do not claim the residual RNA causes impaired recovery. |
| Both immune and recipient RNA differences align with a neural-state shift | Supports a coordinated descriptive pattern; the later survival/output comparison is still unestimated. |
| The apparent signal is predominantly representation change | Develop the abundance/state-transition question using qualified biological units rather than dismissing it or claiming within-state reprogramming. |
| Pattern is mixed, absent or inseparable from combined labels | Weaken this particular RNA-based nomination or hold only the unresolved cell-specific step. The broader neuronal-output question remains legitimate. |

The existing object can support a qualified partial screen; absence of a later
functional assay does not prohibit it. A new numerical contract must first bind
the comparison, exact markers, annotation rules, inputs and exposure. This note
does not claim that the screen has run or that its eligible population is settled.

## Extension 2 and provisional RQ: transient exposure, later neuronal output

**Proposed RQ:** In the aged SVZ–olfactory-bulb lineage, does a defined transient
D25K exposure increase later surviving neuronal output relative to WT IL-10 and
vehicle, and how does that compare with the early progenitor response?

This is a proposed duration/endpoint extension. The nearest Navarro experiment
already measures early DCX/EdU and NeuN/EdU; the increment is later birthdated
survival/maturation after a documented exposure period, with direct ligand
comparisons. It is not a claim that neurogenesis itself was never measured.

The working prediction is a later D25K advantage that is not adequately described
by early progenitor abundance alone. Important alternatives are similar later
output with both ligands, an early-only response, differential survival, and
direct ligand effects on progenitors. These explanations can coexist. Current
neural-marker null comparisons give no license to assume a D25K advantage.

Independent mice are the units; tissue sections and labeled cells are nested.
A qualified birthdating/lineage design and early and later sampling would
separate production, persistence and maturation. Ligand exposure, route, cohort,
age range and sex scope must be explicit and comparable. Counts should retain
absolute output and relevant denominators; a fraction alone can change because
another population changes. Maturation markers are not circuit integration:
physiological integration is needed only if that stronger endpoint is claimed.
No dose, follow-up interval, effect margin or sample size is invented here.

- A sustained D25K–WT difference would support a bounded later-output advantage;
  immune mediation would remain a separate causal question.
- Similar sustained gains for both ligands would favor a common beneficial
  response over a D25K-specific advantage on this endpoint.
- Early increases without later gain would redirect attention to maturation,
  survival or exposure duration; they would not negate the early response.
- Imprecise or heterogeneous differences require their uncertainty and context
  to remain visible, without switching endpoints to obtain a favorable result.

The current deposited snapshots cannot estimate this later outcome. Source
search or new measurement should be driven by this exact missing comparison.
Recipient-specific perturbation becomes justified if the question advances to
mechanism; it is not a compulsory arm for the initial ligand-effect comparison.

## Where aging and existing RQs fit

A separate **age-modification extension** asks whether the D25K–WT response
differs between matched young and old treatment groups. Navarro's young vehicle
reference cannot estimate that interaction. Distinguish an altered T-cell/state
mixture from changed responses within comparable populations; both could matter.
Testing an effect in aged mice alone does not require this factorial design,
but claiming that age changes the effect does. This is a boundary question, not
evidence that another age label is novel.

[A25](../../docs/research_dossiers/A25.md) owns regional microglial RNA
organization against mixture/flexible-age models; it does not own ligand-driven
neural output. [A26](../../docs/research_dossiers/A26.md) owns paired tissue
contrasts in CD8 repertoire aging and [A27](../../docs/research_dossiers/A27.md)
owns repertoire concentration within/between states. None estimates this
neuronal-output contrast. [Sx10-P01](../gate3_07_saxton_2021_il10/README.md)
owns ligand/dose/co-input selectivity comparisons. It supplies premises and
useful contrary patterns without replacing the aged neural-niche question.
Existing identities and evidence remain unchanged; no new global A ID is assigned.
