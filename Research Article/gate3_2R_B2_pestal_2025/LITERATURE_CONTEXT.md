# Tissue competence, subset selection and the KLF2/KLF4 comparison

**Question for interpreting B2.** A precursor can fail to sense a tissue cue, fail to enter or survive in a niche, or mature along a different route. Each would change the cells recovered from a cavity. The within-host competition, transfer, native binding and overexpression experiments address different parts of this problem.

| Primary study and locator | Finding or alternative | Consequence for the planned analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Okabe and Medzhitov (2014), Figs. 2–6 | Macrophage GATA6 deficiency, retinoid perturbation and omental-factor experiments connect retinoic acid to the peritoneal gene program and to localization. Some peritoneal genes respond to additional omental signals. | RA/GATA6 regulation was established before Pestal. Ask what KLF2 adds to competence for that signal and which genes remain outside it. Preserve anatomical redistribution as one explanation for altered lavage abundance. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2014.04.016) |

**External source identity.** Han explicitly reanalyzed GSE225668 for the peritoneal single-cell expression reference. Its GEO record links multiple publications. Han's perturbations and tracing supply the evidence for alternative routes. Replotting the same reference cannot independently replicate its RNA signatures or reveal Pestal’s missing donor histories. Start with the surface-marker combinations and determine whether Pestal measured them, then consider a transcript-level comparison. [GEO record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE225668)

**Implication for B1 and Ravel.** Tissue-specific identity can change the repertoire of receptors available for clearance. The B2 cavity transfer asks how that repertoire is acquired and the lung arm asks which cells remain. B1 measures recognition and inflammatory response to apoptotic cells. Call a cross-paper link mechanistic only to the extent that a matched perturbation and functional endpoint actually test it.
