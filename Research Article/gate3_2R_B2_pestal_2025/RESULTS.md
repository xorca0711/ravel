# Pestal et al. · Cavity acquisition, native occupancy and alveolar identity

## Cavity marker expression after transfer

The source for the cavity RNA plot is the Kallisto estimated fragment counts in nine deposited libraries. All four input libraries had zero estimated fragments for *Timd4*. At day 7, *Timd4* reached 4.92–6.10 log₂(CPM + 1) in the three WT libraries and 2.28–3.40 in the two Klf2-deficient libraries. *Lyve1* rose in both groups and their day-7 ranges overlapped. This pattern supports partial acquisition of cavity traits by recovered deficient cells. Bulk RNA leaves open whether particular cells changed expression or particular subpopulations preferentially survived transfer. ## S1 · KLF2-dependent cavity expression after transfer

Day-7 WT minus KO agrees in sign with Table S2 for **3,251/3,287** shared genes with absolute author log2 fold change ≥1 and nonzero local contrast. Spearman ρ is **0.895** across 12,748 joined genes. The concordance reuses the original study and supports directional reconstruction. It leaves the author normalization, significance and fitted model unreplicated.

Using Table S1 fold change ≥1 in the LCM direction or ≤−1 in the SCM direction and adjusted P ≤0.1, the LCM set had a median WT–KO shift of **−0.020 at day 0 and +0.333 at day 7**. The SCM set had **−0.062 and −0.276**. These are medians of gene-level log2(TPM+1) contrasts over measurable symbols. The released workbook plus these filters does not recover the paper’s exact 643/381 signature sizes. The reported medians describe the fixed workbook-derived sets, with all joins in the effects file. Omitting GSM8763472 solely as a sensitivity changes the day-7 medians to +0.239 and −0.348. Their directions are stable.

## S2 · Which loci fit an upstream KLF2 role

The combined BED on GRCm39 overlaps Gata6, Rara, Rarg and Rxra (2, 9, 5 and 3 peaks, respectively), plus Bhlhe40 and Cebpb (one each). Timd4, Icam2 and Mafb have zero overlaps with the declared gene body plus TSS ±2 kb feature, consistent with the displayed uncalled examples. The feature is an Ensembl release 113 interval and can differ from a browser panel's boundaries. Rarb has one overlap under this rule and should be distinguished from the paper's displayed Rara locus. A call gives occupancy context without assigning the regulatory element to a target or establishing its necessity.

KLF2-versus-empty and GATA6-versus-empty reproduced 2,506/2,519 and 2,048/2,064 strong-author/nonzero local signs, respectively. Rarg and Rxra met the author adjusted P ≤0.1 and log2 FC ≥1 with KLF2 but did not meet both thresholds with GATA6. Rarb was positive with both under those criteria, with larger local and author estimates for KLF2. Timd4 and Icam2 combined day-7 dependence with induction after KLF2 overexpression and zero declared peak overlaps. Mafb showed KLF2 induction but weak day-7 dependence. Both overexpression arms induced Serpinb2, Hp and Tgfb2 by author thresholds. Hp lacked a usable Table S2 entry and had zero declared overlaps. Tgfb2 had day-7 dependence with zero declared overlaps. Serpinb2 had two overlaps and weaker day-7 adjusted evidence. These differences limit a single ordering across genes.

In Fig. 5D–F, added all-trans retinoic acid changed KLF2-transduced BMMs, with little reported shift for empty-vector or GATA6-transduced BMMs in the projection. KLF2 plus retinoic acid enhanced ICAM2, LYVE1 and TIM-4, and increased GATA6 protein in the KLF2 condition. The in-vitro response, occupancy and transfer RNA together support retinoid competence as part of the phenotype. They are distinct preparations. ## S4 · Alternative cavity routes

Under the Han marker combinations, the recovered KO looked less like the mature CD73/TIMD4/GATA6-positive LCM3 state. Day-7 WT–KO was +0.79 for Nt5e, +2.79 for Timd4 and +2.37 for Gata6. Lyve1 favored KO (−0.61) at day 7 after rising strongly in both genotypes, with substantial library variation. Icam2 strongly favored WT at day 7 even though it rose in KO. The mixed pattern and lack of joint marker observations prevent assigning the KO to Han's ICAM2-positive LCM2b route or establishing mesothelial diversion. Pestal's published transfer flow distinguishes the broad ICAM2 and MHCII phenotype. It supplies no joint subset classification equivalent to Han's Fig. 1E–G and no lineage readout. Han's Fig. 3 tracing also establishes that LYVE1-positive intermediates are dispensable for most LCM3 in that model. This weakens a simple linear interpretation without choosing between altered acquisition and selection in Pestal. ## S5 · Tissue-specific conclusions

**Cavity.** KLF2 loss changes both LCM recovery and the phenotype of the recovered compartment. SCM contribution within the same host is better preserved, and the day-7 expression gap is larger than at day 0. Native occupancy and the retinoic acid challenge support a KLF2-dependent competence component alongside altered population recovery. To resolve their relative contributions, compare input-matched precursors within the same host, recording viable donor yield independently of markers and measuring joint acquisition in the surviving cells. Timd4 and Mertk connect the LCM state to apoptotic-cell recognition and uptake, and the KO-enriched Tlr9 direction reported in Fig. 3 connects to the nucleic-acid sensing question. Roberts tested corpse-triggered sensing and KLF2-related regulation in that setting. Pestal did not measure the corresponding uptake or sensing endpoint in the transfer perturbation, so an identity shift here cannot establish a change in clearance or signaling. [Pestal et al.](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20240379)

**Lung.** KLF4 loss is associated with lower AM recovery and competitive representation, together with changes in the residual AM gate. Several residual RNA markers show clear agreement with Table S5. Others disagree with the flow direction or are nearly unchanged. The unresolved steps are whether genotype affects entry, survival, or maturation within the gate, and whether the residual changes affect AM function. [Separate lung evidence](S3_LUNG.md)
