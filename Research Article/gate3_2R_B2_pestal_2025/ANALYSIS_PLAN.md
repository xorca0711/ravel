# B2 · How does a broadly expressed KLF confer a tissue-specific macrophage identity?

**Organizing question.** The peritoneum supplies retinoid signals, yet only some incoming macrophages acquire a large cavity macrophage (LCM) identity. Does KLF2 make precursors competent to interpret that niche, does it chiefly favor recovery of an already competent subset, or do both contribute? The lung gives a second question of equal biological interest: how does KLF4 loss affect entry into the alveolar macrophage (AM) pool and the phenotype of cells that remain? These are two tissue-specific developmental comparisons. The second is especially relevant to Ravel's interest in lung macrophages. [Pestal et al., Figs. 1–7 and Tables S1–S5](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20240379)

**What the study already contributes.** In mixed chimeras, mutant and competitor cells encounter the same host. In the transfer experiment, a standard precursor population can be examined before and after niche entry. In native LCMs, binding at retinoid receptor loci and *Gata6* gives a possible order to transcriptional events. The residual cellular phenotypes of Klf2 and Gata6 deficiency differ. Together these findings warrant asking which parts of identity require upstream KLF2, which can be induced by GATA6, and which differences could reflect changed representation. [Literature on RA, branching cavity lineages and lung identity](LITERATURE_CONTEXT.md)
