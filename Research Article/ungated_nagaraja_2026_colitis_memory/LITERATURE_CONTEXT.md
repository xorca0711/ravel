# Literature and deposit record

**Scope, 19 November 2026.** Read the final Nature paper, Figs. 1–4, Extended Data and relevant supplementary notes alongside the owner's completed Results and Discussion reading. The published direction of the AP-1, clone and tumour comparisons was known before planning. Search coverage included primary articles on mouse and human intestinal memory, a skin precedent, and clonal selection as an alternative in human colitis. Search terms included “Nagaraja epigenetic memory of colitis”, “human colonic organoids inflammatory memory chromatin accessibility”, “intestinal regenerative inflammatory memory Arozamena”, and “somatic inflammatory gene mutations human ulcerative colitis epithelium”. Public repositories checked were the journal supplement, IGVF, GEO and the archived author code. This was a targeted feasibility scan; no numerical analysis was run. ### What S1 recovered from Figure 1

Nagaraja et al. already reported higher FOS staining at early recovery alongside a stem-cell motif shift and attenuation of the injury transcriptional response (Fig. 1d,e,g,k,l). The [S1 result](s1/RESULTS.md) measures the staining difference from those same mice (+16.42 percentage points) and reads the deposited stage and state summaries. The released Table 1 thresholds give a 260-gene injury union versus the published 246. The 14 exclusion identities and many recovery adjusted P values are unresolved. This shared-source readback supports the protein contrast and qualifies the RNA interpretation. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13083248/#Fig1)

## Reconciling the organoid release with Fig. 2

The published Fig. 2h Table 4 variances and the Fig. 2i high-score fractions can be traced to different clone groupings. The S2 replay found that Fos-family resemblance under the marginal shuffle weakens when analysis is restricted to unambiguous cells. The Hnf4g comparison remains strong. The six mouse lines overlap in mean Fos score, and the originally fitted Fig. 2j P20 correlation remains dependent on unreleased matched score inputs. These findings narrow the case for a persistent AP-1-associated subset while leaving differentiation and line composition unresolved. [S2 results](s2/RESULTS.md) · [Original Fig. 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13083248/#Fig2)

The [S3 readback](s3/RESULTS.md) separates defined-protein footprint scores in the selected regions from predicted tissue occupancy. At two illustrated whole-colon windows, the mean methylation difference remaining after the inhibitor varied by locus. The original peak-set comparison and organoid accessibility–methylation correlation remain author estimates. [Original Extended Data Fig. 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13083248/#Fig10)
