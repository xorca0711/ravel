# Does JUN–FOXP1 keep colonic memory sites open after FOS declines?

**Literature and public-deposit review, 20 November 2026.** The public data support separate tests of FOS-independent binding, intrinsic methylation sensitivity and persistence of accessibility. I found no recovery-stage colonic epithelial dataset combining direct JUN and FOXP1 occupancy, site-resolved methylation, accessibility and a perturbation that separates their direction of dependence. Both mechanisms could act in succession or reinforce each other.

## Which independent studies help discriminate the explanations?

- **Methylation sensitivity of each factor.** Yin et al. deposited ordinary HT-SELEX and methylated HT-SELEX for the human FOXP1 extended DNA-binding domain and full-length JUN. ENA lists selection cycles 1–4 in each condition. Verified cycle-4 records are ERX1082408/09 (FOXP1) and ERX1083384/85 (JUN), ordinary/methylated respectively. Compare sequence preferences at specified CpG positions, taking account of starting libraries and selection cycle. Selection cycles are successive enrichments, not independent replicates. These are separate protein preparations, without the JUN–FOXP1 complex or colonic chromatin. No direction of preference was inferred from sample titles. Gustems et al. found that methylation can actually favor JUN–FOS at a class of CpG-containing AP-1 sequences. This makes sequence and dimer identity essential to interpreting a methylation difference. [Yin et al., Fig. 1 and Table S3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8009048/) · [PRJEB9797](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/PRJEB9797) · [Gustems et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkt1323)
- **Occupancy versus necessity after FOS declines.** In mouse epidermal stem cells, Larsen et al. detected JUN at day-30 memory sites after nuclear FOS declined. Delaying dominant-negative AP-1 inhibition until after memory establishment left the sites accessible. ATF3 remained bound in the accompanying keratinocyte experiment. GEO contains D30 JUN CUT&RUN (GSM5229104–07) and D30 A-FOS ATAC (GSM5229077–80). Cowley et al. separately provide one-year epidermal JUN CUT&RUN (two pools per history), methylation and histone profiles including H2A.Z. Their FOS CUT&RUN at one year involves TPA restimulation. CpG sequence, demethylation, nucleosome properties and histone composition can contribute to persistence. These are skin precedents and give a reason to distinguish a factor that marks a site from one required to keep it open in colon. [Larsen et al., Fig. 5A–G](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8500942/) · [GSE171596](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE171596) · [Cowley et al., Figs. 4–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13295011/) · [JUN CUT&RUN](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE299178) · [Bisulfite sequencing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE299606) · [Histone ChIP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE300003)
- **An independent colon methylation perturbation has limited genomic overlap.** GSE283386 measures colonic epithelial DNA from 12 mice (three each under vehicle, DSS, demethylating treatment or both), 14 days after DSS withdrawal. Drug exposure began before DSS and continued after withdrawal. Eight measured, unmasked CpG probes overlap **five of the 63** Nagaraja amplicons. **Zero overlap the 63 scored motif windows.** Of S3's three illustrated windows, only *Thbs1* had probes (two). These measured probe IDs support a regional, cross-study check if their sample-level data qualify. This array supplies no JUN/FOXP1 occupancy, exact motif methylation, or recovery-only intervention. [GSE283386 design and beta matrix](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE283386) · [GPL30650 mm10 manifest](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GPL30650) · [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12873232/)
- **Other intestinal data distinguish model and stage.** Ansari et al. provide colon epithelial bisulfite data for conventional/germ-free mice with and without DSS. The six ATAC samples specify DSS or untreated, three per arm, without individual cross-assay IDs established here. This tests injury-associated methylation/accessibility concordance. Hamdan’s paired human UC organoids provide accessibility and transcription after culture. Arozamena examines intestinal regeneration after irradiation. Neither supplies paired late-colon JUN/FOXP1 occupancy and methylation. In a separate mechanistic precedent, Feldmann et al. found that loss of REST in embryonic stem cells altered methylation turnover at its binding sites, illustrating the reverse direction, occupancy influencing the methylation mark. [Ansari et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-019-0659-3) · [Bisulfite GSE125978](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE125978) · [ATAC GSE141458](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE141458) · [Hamdan et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcmgh.2026.101774) · [Arozamena et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79014-6) · [Feldmann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1003994)

## The next informative public-data comparison

1. **Anchor the comparison on the 63 published intervals.** Use each locus's within-sequence JUN + FOXP1 versus JUN-alone footprint contrast as biochemical evidence already exposed by the paper. Keep AP-1-only and composite classes separate. Qualify the actual reference CpGs within the motif and in prespecified flanking intervals, native methylation coverage per specimen, and eligible late-recovery ATAC signal by mouse or labelled preparation. Record sites without motif CpGs explicitly. Regional demethylation can influence chromatin without changing a directly contacted base. The three previously illustrated loci answer a separate question.
2. **Ask whether biochemical cooperation tracks persistent accessibility and methylation loss.** Test whether sites with stronger FOS-free cooperation retain more accessibility at 50–102 days and whether their recovered native methylation is lower. Account for baseline accessibility, CpG content, motif class and the original selection of the 63 sequences. Report organoid and whole-colon associations separately. Use animals for history contrasts and loci for sequence associations. Coverage, mouse assignment and eligible cell states must qualify before numerical execution. A positive result would prioritize compatible loci. A null result in this selected panel would limit generalization. Neither outcome demonstrates occupancy or establishes which change happened first.
3. **Use the Yin data for the specific alternative.** Where the tested sequence contexts overlap, ask whether methylating a motif CpG changes the isolated factor’s binding preference in the same direction. Direct dependence of the *pair* requires a FOS-free JUN–FOXP1 comparison on otherwise identical methylated and unmethylated templates. Direct maintenance in colon requires late factor-specific occupancy plus a recovery-stage change in factor binding or methylation, with subsequent accessibility measured in comparable epithelial cells. Comparing reciprocal changes would distinguish necessity, upstream permission, feedback and passive persistence. S4’s adenoma cohort does not link these alternatives to lesion growth.
