# Song et al. (2024) — An NAD+-dependent metabolic checkpoint regulates hematopoietic stem cell activation and aging

**gate3_2R_C2** · Brief owner read reported 26 November 2026. Revised descriptive analysis executed and verified 26 November 2026.

## Published findings

```text
Young HSCs under activation → CD38 supports mitochondrial calcium signaling and proliferation
Aged HSCs → higher CD38 accompanies impaired NAD+ homeostasis
    → aged Cd38-deficient donors show improved repopulation
    → pharmacological CD38 inhibition improves selected aged-mouse endpoints
```

Young HSCs show little overt homeostatic phenotype in the reported assays, yet proliferate less after CD38 loss under activation. The paper measures calcium, metabolites and proliferation separately (Figs. 1–2). Aged knockout donors and treatment of aged mice provide distinct evidence (Figs. 3–4). [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-024-00670-8)

## Ravel analyses and results

Young Cd38 KO HSCs after cytokines showed lower cell recovery and BrdU incorporation, with little separation in 7-AAD staining. MCU knockdown nearly removed the Rhod-2 genotype difference while colony output retained a KO deficit with sensitivity to individual observations. Week-16 donor output was 30.77 percentage points lower in recipients of young Cd38 KO donor cells and 11.43 points higher in the aged cohort. Aged Cd38 loss and systemic 78c both increased relative NAD+ and lowered stress-sensitive signals. ## Data and methods

GSE268310 provides 12 mouse enriched-HSC RNA libraries. Both genotypes appear in young fresh, young cytokine-stimulated and old fresh samples. The deposited old animals have no stimulated RNA group. Figures 1f and 3g report peripheral-blood donor contribution after competitive transplantation. [RNA deposit](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE268310)

## Interpretation and open questions

Less division after stimulation and improved long-term output from aged deficient donors can coexist. Calcium, cycling and survival measurements distinguish explanations for the young phenotype. The enriched sort and systemic inhibitor exposure constrain the interpretation for aged HSCs. A published CD38–SIRT3 relationship in liver leaves the corresponding stem-cell mechanism open.
