# C2 · Why does CD38 support young HSC activation yet accompany poorer regeneration in old age?

**Organizing question.** A quiescent HSC must activate mitochondrial metabolism to divide. With age, NAD+ availability and the ability to manage mitochondrial stress also matter for subsequent regeneration. Can the released experiments distinguish a benefit of CD38-dependent calcium signaling during young-cell activation from a cost of sustained CD38 activity in older HSCs? Which part of the model is supported by the MCU perturbation, and which part still needs a matched metabolic and regenerative measurement? [Song et al., Figs. 1–4 and Extended Data Figs. 1–10](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-024-00670-8)

**Competing explanations.** Young CD38 loss might reduce entry into cycle or survival. An age difference in donor contribution could arise from stem/progenitor composition, homing or the transplantation setting. Systemic inhibitor treatment also changes the environment surrounding HSCs. A direct effect on mitochondrial calcium and a separate benefit from preserving NAD+ need not have the same sign at every age. The proposed reproduction follows the paper’s perturbations and direct measurements before using RNA to characterize state. [Metabolic and aging literature](LITERATURE_CONTEXT.md)
