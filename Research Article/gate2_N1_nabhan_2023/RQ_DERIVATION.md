## The biological problem that survives the analyses

The repository asks what separates productive repair from persistent remodeling.
Nb2 sharpens that problem: **an input that expands a repair-associated population
need not preserve its later contribution to a functional alveolus**. Receptor
abundance, selected RNA outputs, population growth and mature tissue contribution
must be connected in the same biological units before they explain that distinction.
This is an inference from the combined evidence, not an observed common mechanism.

The [first-pass results](RESULTS.md), [branch results](branch_analysis/RESULTS.md)
and [measurement specification](branch_analysis/METHODS.md) supply the evidence.
The table records observations that change the questions, including unfavorable
results from the original branches.

source units whose animal/preparation identities still bound inference. These
data cannot serve again as untouched validation.

## Biological framing rule

The owner requires each RQ to be plausibly framed as a biological hypothesis.
Here the causal ideas are **a reversible expansion-to-maturation transition**,
**receptor control of a trophic fibroblast state**, and **recruitment into capillary
renewal**. Expression discordance and round effects motivate rivals and controls;
they are not the questions themselves. Each mechanism is a post-analysis proposal
whose functional predictions go beyond the current measurements.

## Three questions, with different priorities

| Proposed RQ | Question and hypothesis | Derivation | First discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [A19](../../RQ_Specified/A19_fzd_response_reversibility/README.md), primary | Can receptor-selective Wnt stimulation expand AT2 cells while retaining later mature AT1 contribution? A verified off-period improves mature descendant output while preserving a responsive AT2 pool; starting state and input may modify that effect | D1–D4; combine N1/N2/N3/N5 because the later biological endpoint is shared, with separate duration, state and output decisions | Within-preparation exposure history linked to independent later mature output, survival and remaining regenerative capacity; validated initial states for the interaction |
| [A20](../../RQ_Specified/A20_fibroblast_fzd_context/README.md), secondary | Do subtype-specific Fzd signals maintain a niche that enables epithelial maturation? Fzd2 sustains AF1-like trophic activity, with receptor dependence varying by state; matrix remodeling remains a separate output | D5; replace the unique-benefit formulation of N6 with a receptor-by-subtype question | Subtype-resolved, receptor-specific perturbation with epithelial mature output and actual matrix measurements |
| [A21](../../RQ_Specified/A21_fzd4_capillary_function/README.md), conditional | Does Fzd4 recruit gCap cells into regenerative renewal after injury? Receptor-dependent state entry supplies gCap/aerocyte descendants; general maintenance is the competing mechanism | D6; retain N7 as a challenge to progenitor specificity, with weaker priority after the round audit | Independent within-state replication for the expression lead; receptor dependency linked to lineage output and survival/performance for the mechanism |

A19 has three separable hypotheses: one successful test does not establish the
others. A21 may be informative even if renewal specificity fails: maintenance
alone is a biological alternative. Failed expression replication closes that
discovery claim, without proving that Fzd4 has no function.

## Relationship to existing RQs

A19 changes the **input and its temporal history** and measures later lineage
output. A4 owns Wnt-history/IL-1 sequence; A8 owns added predictive information in
a maturation component; A10 owns RNA–growth relationships; A1 owns regulatory
state/response distinctions. They supply endpoints or rivals without being
renamed. A14's duration design concerns IL-1 and remains separate. The Nb2 AT1
RNA panel is not A8's independent outcome.

A20 intervenes on fibroblast Fzd context. A13 asks about added predictive
information beyond macrophage IL1B; A15 concerns epithelial integrin/TGF-beta
input. Neither tests the new receptor-by-subtype contrast. A21 adds a vascular
recipient and lineage outcome; vascular RNA is not an epithelial repair endpoint.
Cross-compartment convergence requires linked units/outcomes, not favorable
signatures in unrelated cohorts.

## Source grounding and next work

[Nabhan et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37321220/) supplies the receptor-
selective regeneration context. [Gaona et al.](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/198113)
supplies a distinct experimental setting in which sustained YAP/TAZ activation
alters repair. Its Stk3/4-loss reference is neither purified TEAD activity nor
a persistence classifier; it cannot establish a Fzd-specific mechanism.

The [Fzd2 study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12964-025-02501-8)
provides a mesenchymal perturbation comparator for A20. Its published Fzd2 role is
prior art; neither Fzd2 involvement nor the AF1/AF2 distinction is claimed as new.
The specified addition is a within-subtype receptor comparison with separate
functional outcomes. [Gillich et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7721049/)
supplies gCap lineage motivation for A21, without demonstrating Fzd4 dependency
in Nb2's atlas. This bounded check is not an exhaustive novelty or dataset search.

Proceed first with an **eligibility inventory for A19**, linking source identity,
initial state, engagement/withdrawal history and later endpoints. A20 needs an
assay/subtype crosswalk for the Fzd2 comparator before fitting. A21 needs cohort
and capillary-substate identifiers before expanding correlations. Each
[question plan](../../RQ_Specified/README.md) defines comparisons and stop rules.
Margins, assay choices and validation cohorts remain unset; these proposed
contracts do not authorize confirmatory scoring.
