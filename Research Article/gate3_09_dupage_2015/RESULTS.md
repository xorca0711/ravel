Figures 4–5 and the lineage component of 7 support identity instability; the
reported later apoptosis also supports a survival contribution. Those explanations
can coexist. Lower tissue frequency does **not** demonstrate impaired homing.
The short-term CD62L-high suppression assay in Figure 2F does not establish
preserved activated-tissue function. The RNA dataset cannot connect a specific
gene or chromatin domain to any of these outcomes.

**Held numerical branch:** no qualified raw FCS/event files, per-mouse absolute
viable counts, paired lineage/viability series or longitudinal EAE trajectories
were recovered through the inspected paper/GEO/supplement routes. Individual
mouse IDs, repeated-measurement structure, cohort/allocation and missingness
are required before reanalysis. This is an access/qualification limit, not a
claim that the authors never collected the data. Mean curves were not digitized
into fictitious animals, and assays from different cohorts were not pooled.

**Checkpoint:** identity and survival remain jointly plausible; trafficking
and RNA-to-disease mediation remain unmeasured. This endpoint map changes the
next question from a generic Treg-stability score to a comparison that explicitly
keeps viable cell number, lineage-marker retention and function separate.

## S6 — defensible next scientific decision

The cascade remains:

**CD28 stimulation → Ezh2-dependent regulation during activation → Foxp3-associated
programme → identity/survival in tissues → inflammatory control.**

The first link is a published premise (Figure 1), not tested by GSE58998. This
run supplies shared-source descriptive RNA contrasts and limited external
chromatin consistency for the second/third links. Foxp3-associated reference
replay remains partial. Published lineage, apoptosis and EAE experiments address
later endpoints in separate cohorts. The arrows connecting a particular RNA/
chromatin change to identity, survival or inflammatory control remain proposed.

**Current disposition:** retain an exposed source reconstruction with mixed
state-dependence and weak covered-subset chromatin consistency. No specific
direct target, universal methylation rule, new mechanism or canonical RQ is
justified by this run. This is an analytical conclusion, not an invented human
retain/reject decision or claim-grade promotion.

**Exact next scientific question, proposed for owner review:** under the same
defined activation exposure, do Ezh2-dependent regulatory changes appear within
viable lineage-labelled Tregs before shifts in Foxp3 retention or selective cell
loss? A matched HET/KO comparison would need direct chromatin/RNA readouts,
Foxp3 retention among viable lineage-labelled cells, absolute viable numbers,
division and death measured with compatible timing. Timing is needed if ordering
is the intended inference. A relevant suppression endpoint is needed only if
the intended claim includes function; direct tracking is needed for migration.
No rescue, extra group or catalytic control is automatically mandated.

Before turning that question into an experiment package, recover the original
reference selections and mouse/pool crosswalk where possible, then specify the
candidate endpoint, model, allocation and measurement feasibility. A target,
effect margin, precision/sample-size basis and laboratory resources remain
unresolved; none were fabricated. The present sparse descriptive associations
do not nominate one gene as a validated target. A future count re-quantification
