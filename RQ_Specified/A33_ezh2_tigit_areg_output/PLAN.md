## Decision and comparison

Wang lower Tigit/Prdm1 with higher Areg makes basal RNA a particularly poor proxy for an evoked output. Keep the smaller-response working hypothesis as a testable prediction; specify baseline and inducible increments separately. The published TIGIT/Blimp-1/AREG mechanism remains a premise, not the proposed discovery. [Wang interpretation](../../Research%20Article/gate3_10_wang_2018_ezh2/BIOLOGICAL_INTERPRETATION_V1.md) · [Literature and extension review](../../Research%20Article/gate3_10_wang_2018_ezh2/RQ_FOLLOWUP_REVIEW_20261112.md).

The immediate decision is whether the engagement-minus-control AREG response differs between genotypes under a common responsive activation context. A retained response would narrow the interpretation toward context or expression differences; an output deficit would justify asking whether receptor availability, general cell fitness or PRDM1 contributes. Measuring a genotype response effect does not require proving the entire pathway first.

Common TCR/co-stimulation; TIGIT engagement versus matched reagent control in aliquots from Ezh2-deficient and matched-control preparations. Estimate the genotype difference in engagement-minus-control AREG release, retaining the two simple responses and baseline output.

Here baseline means the matched reagent-control output under the same activation and collection window, not an interchangeable unstimulated measurement. The primary contrast is (deficient engagement − deficient control) − (genetic-control engagement − genetic-control control). A smaller increment with higher basal output need not mean lower total AREG release.

## Stages

| Stage | Required evidence or comparison | Stop, qualify or narrow when |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish comparison | Lineage identity, actual Ezh2 allele/control, common activation history and accessory-cell source; linked viable input and recovery. | Record independent animals/pools and preparation IDs; do not turn HET into WT or use present FOXP3 alone as ancestry. |
| 2. Qualify response | Surface TIGIT and a responsive AREG release assay, with reagent/Fc, background and source controls. | If genetic controls do not respond, the assay cannot test the smaller-response prediction; absent KO response may itself be the outcome. |
| 3. Fix and estimate output contrast | Engagement minus control within preparation, then the genotype difference in that response; retain baseline output, total AREG and recovery. | Freeze endpoint/timing, allocation, assay range and biological-unit precision. Report an opposite direction as well as a smaller or unchanged response. |
| 4. Decide extension | Assess basal-output/response dissociation and recovery/activation alternatives. | Add a context interaction only for a specified boundary question; PRDM1 mediation and recipient repair are separate comparisons. |

These are design decisions, not a compulsory sequence of separate studies. Source and assay qualification may be embedded in the same experiment. A genotype effect can be informative without an extra rescue; additional perturbations are justified by a target-attribution claim.

## Model choice and linked measurements

Specify whether the comparison concerns longstanding genetic loss or acute perturbation of established Tregs. Record starting tissue/state, driver or exposure timing, the interval before activation and readout, and actual control background. Either can support its stated comparison; neither automatically estimates the other. Verify the intended perturbation using the appropriate allele/recombination and molecular evidence. A claim about catalytic inhibition requires a qualified target-engagement readout; Ezh2 gene-level RNA alone does not provide it. Wang Figure S4D supplies an inducible-loss/H3K27me3 precedent in already sorted Tregs, and Peeters Figure 4 supplies a separate inducible-gain precedent. Neither qualifies the actual response preparation or turns the existing constitutive deposits into an acute experiment. Exact implementation and timing remain unresolved. Direct-target chromatin assays are not prerequisites for the present functional question.

If interpreting RNA–protein–response discordance, link the measurements to the same biological preparation, assigned condition and sampling window through nested aliquot IDs. Different studies or unlinked preparations cannot establish post-transcriptional regulation. This linkage is required for that interpretation, not for a standalone qualified response comparison. Preserve recovery and state composition as measured outcomes; conditioning on recovered cells does not recover a direct genotype effect.

The [neighbor-paper review](../../Research%20Article/gate3_10_wang_2018_ezh2/RQ_FOLLOWUP_REVIEW_20261112.md#neighboring-article-flow-and-revisited-gaps) explains why these specifications matter and which requirements were already present.

## Biological unit and readout

Independent mouse or independently identified source pool is the intended biological unit. Repeated preparations from the same animal, aliquots, wells and cells are nested; they do not add independent animals. Pooling and pairing must be recorded explicitly. Fix the actual genetic control, common exposure and preparation/accessory-source structure; allocate aliquots and blind readouts where feasible.

Secreted AREG, assay background, absolute viable recovery and supportive TIGIT/Blimp-1 protein measurements. AREG per viable cell and total recovered output answer different questions; report both when interpretable.

Source tissue, age/sex, allele/control, timing, assay range, biological-unit variance and meaningful precision remain unresolved. Do not substitute an arbitrary minimum sample count for a power or precision rationale.

Record total released AREG per standardized starting preparation over the fixed collection window, reagent-control baseline and both within-genotype responses. Report absolute recovered viable lineage counts and, where interpretable, output per recovered cell as a distinct conditional quantity. Final-cell normalization cannot reconstruct cumulative secretion per living cell or remove differential recovery. Preserve paired aliquot IDs; distinguish biological pools from technical pooling.

Operationally, the AREG release endpoint is net extracellular amount recovered over the collection window. Record medium volume and background. Degradation, uptake and harvest affect this amount; without additional validated measurements it is not a secretion rate.

## Informative outcomes

A smaller KO response with comparable recovery supports an output deficit in the qualified assay. A comparable response weakens that prediction without negating the RNA result. A deficit tracking broad activation failure or cell loss leaves a general fitness account. If neither control responds, the assay cannot test the genotype prediction.

Different activation history, receptor availability, cell selection or loss, and accessory/Fc effects can alter the response. A response deficit does not alone identify PRDM1 mediation.

## Conditional extension

**Activation context as an effect modifier.** Panetti already shows activation-dependent TIGIT/AREG output (Figures 5–6, Extended Data 8); the extension is whether Ezh2 changes that dependence. Wang/Peeters RNA discordance motivates the question but does not identify activation as its cause.

After a qualified response comparison, choose two biologically justified activation/priming conditions with documented assay responsiveness and allocate aliquots from the same source preparations. Retain genotype × engagement comparisons within each condition. The extension estimand is the change across conditions in the genotype difference of engagement-minus-control AREG release. Timing and conditions remain to be qualified, rather than copied from unlike source studies.

A context-dependent genotype difference would restrict transfer of the initial result; a similarly estimated difference across the two contexts would support transfer only across those tested contexts. Higher basal AREG with a smaller increment would show output/inducibility dissociation. Recovery differences and post-perturbation cell composition remain part of the biology and interpretation, not covariates that automatically remove selection. Neither outcome proves PRDM1 mediation. If source or response qualification fails, stop this extension and retain that result.

### Optional bridge to conventional suppression

After the AREG response assay and a defined suppression assay are qualified, aliquots from the same independently sourced Treg preparations could test whether Ezh2 loss affects these outputs together. Use a specified conventional-T-cell responder population with common input, matched stimulation and responder-only controls; measure responder division or another prespecified suppression endpoint, Treg input and recovery. Retain TIGIT engagement-minus-control AREG output as A33's primary comparison. The suppression comparison adds a distinct genotype effect and does not substitute for that endpoint.

Retained AREG responsiveness with weaker suppression, weaker AREG with retained suppression, or changes in both would each constrain the functional interpretation. Specify precision for each endpoint; a significant effect in one assay and a nonsignificant effect in another does not demonstrate selectivity. Common source preparations do not make different assay environments identical or prove both behaviors occur in the same cell. Panetti already distinguishes tissue-protective and immunoregulatory functions; the proposed increment is their relationship under the specified Ezh2 perturbation. AREG accumulation is not a recipient repair measurement. This optional bridge does not require a new RQ, replace the activation-context extension, or become a prerequisite for the primary study.
