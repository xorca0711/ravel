# A22 rationale: epithelial identity as an input to niche immune function

This is a post-analysis hypothesis dated 1 October 2026, not an unseen-data
prediction or a novelty claim. [Nb3](../../Research%20Article/gate2_N2_nabhan_2026/README.md)
supplies the exposed screen observations; the question asks what causes them.

## Biological proposition

An alveolar epithelium may regulate its neighbouring fibroblasts through its
differentiated state as well as its size. In particular, loss of NKX2-1-dependent
identity may lower fibroblast chemokine competence while increasing a
wound-associated response. A coordinated change in recipient function would
connect epithelial lineage state to niche immune organization.

This connection is plausible, but not established by the screen. Spatial
human-lung work describes a chemokine-rich fibroblast niche associated with
immune structures ([Madissoon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01243-4)).
Its anatomical setting and chemokines differ from Nb3; it supplies context,
not replication of an NKX2-1 effect. The source
[Nabhan study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2606113123) supplies the specific
epithelial-perturbation context.

## Observation-to-hypothesis chain

1. NKX21 combines a broad epithelial identity change with lower chemokine and
   higher wound-marker RNA in the separately assigned human compartment.
2. In the additional analysis, all seven chemokine contrasts are negative.
   Control substitution and single-marker/well omission do not reverse the
   aggregate phenotype. It is therefore not readily explained by one chosen
   chemokine or one NKX21 well in these diagnostics.
3. Neither growth association nor read-depth conditioning isolates an identity
   mechanism. The cross-target relationship is modest, and pathway q values
   are highly sensitive to intergene-correlation assumptions.
4. The appropriate next proposition concerns a selective, identity-linked
   recipient response. It does not name TNF, interferon, AREG or TGF-beta as a
   demonstrated mediator. Lower chemokine RNA also does not mean global
   immunosuppression or resistance to immunotherapy.

The competing explanations are changed epithelial/fibroblast abundance,
different epithelial or fibroblast state mixtures, target/guide/position effects,
and a shared culture-stress response. RNA amount is a possible biological
intermediate as well as a measurement concern. Adjusting it away cannot decide
which explanation is true.

## Distinct scope

A10 predicts measured growth from epithelial programmes. A13 asks whether
fibroblast programmes add information about epithelial plasticity. A22 instead
tests epithelial identity as an input to fibroblast chemokine and recipient
function. A2/A15's ligand-delivery and integrin/TGF-beta mechanisms are possible
comparators, not answers inherited by this question. E1 and E8 contribute one
shared motivation; lung-specific fibroblast identity is a separate untested
claim because PLIN2 alone cannot define it.
