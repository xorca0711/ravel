# From published findings to a research question

Use this workflow when deriving a question from a reproduction/branch result,
materially changing its hypothesis or endpoint, preparing its next experiment
package, or finding a close new precedent. The aim is to develop an informative
question from existing knowledge. Replication, boundary tests and adjudicating
competing explanations can be worthwhile contributions.

## Start with the actual observation

Read the current result/amendment and dossier through the registry. Record the
published finding, what the reproduction or branch actually measured, the
negative or inconsistent results, and what is still unexplained. Label outcome
exposure. Reanalysis of source data and citation of the original paper are one
evidence lineage, not independent replication.

Write a comparison tuple: cell/population, tissue/model, exposure or perturbation,
timing, comparator, measured endpoint and biological unit. An unknown stays
unknown. The tuple helps distinguish an exact precedent from a related mechanism;
it is not a requirement to invent an intervention before defining the question.

## Search beyond the current bibliography

1. Search the biological relationship and synonyms broadly, including related
   cell contexts. Do not restrict searches to the target PIs or downloaded corpus.
2. Search the exact proposed comparison and strongest rival. Include terms for
   negative, contradictory and boundary findings where relevant.
3. Inspect recent primary work since the last recorded search, and older closest
   antecedents. On a first pass, use a stated recent window (normally the past
   two years) alongside unrestricted foundational searches. Record publication
   date, online date and version; date filters can miss records.
4. Follow references and citing papers from the closest primary studies. Report
   which citation directions/databases were actually inspected. Reviews and
   search summaries are discovery aids, not the final basis for a biological claim.
5. Read the primary Results, figure legends, relevant methods and available
   supplements for the exact overlap. Check corrections/retractions and linked
   versions. Separate a preprint from its later article and avoid double counting.

Use live search when available. Record actual queries, service, date, filters,
coverage, access failures and stopping point. If live access is unavailable,
mark the review incomplete and preserve the existing interpretation limit.
An abstract-only or indexed-text check is not a full-figure/methods audit.
No finite search proves that all published literature has been exhausted.

## Explain how each finding changes the question

For each close primary source, retain a DOI or stable URL and precise locator.
State what was measured, in which context/unit, and what it contributes here:
the premise, a rival explanation, an endpoint-validity constraint, a candidate
source/model, a contradiction, or an extension worth testing. Record shared
datasets/animals and avoid treating paper count as independent support.

Then trace **published finding → repository observation → unresolved contrast →
proposed comparison (hypothesis and alternatives where applicable) → informative outcomes**. State what the possible results would
teach beyond the nearest published experiment. A changed gene label, dataset or
age bin is not by itself a scientific increment; explain why the changed biology
or comparison matters. Distinct tissues and cell populations can justify distinct
questions without certifying novelty.

Use an explicit contribution description: replication, measurement validation,
model discrimination, context/boundary extension, mechanistic extension, already
directly tested, or unresolved specification. These are descriptions, not a ranking
or an automatic retain/reject decision. A published mechanism can remain a premise
for a useful question. Do not turn absence of a search hit into “novel” or “first.”

## Save the argument where the question is read

Use the [context template](templates/LITERATURE_CONTEXT.md). In a dedicated RQ
folder maintain `LITERATURE_CONTEXT.md`; questions with existing article/shared
workspaces use dossier companions and a link from that source entrypoint. Keep
article-local candidates in their article namespace until authorized registration.

Update the context, dossier established-knowledge/gap discussion, local README
and central index together when their meaning changes. Change the canonical
hypothesis only when a justified substantive revision is actually being adopted;
show the prior premise and reason, preserve results and prospective amendments,
and do not manufacture human acceptance. Link the context from the next numerical
contract's `evidence_refs`; all existing eligibility/exposure rules still apply.

Use an explanatory schematic when it clarifies the contrast. Include the full
question or hypothesis, relevant alternative/validity threat, readout-to-decision mapping, evidence limits and
missing specifications. Mark proposed arrows and qualitative counts. A pretty
diagram must not convert a score into fate, secretion or function.

Keep the existing template headings for navigation and validation. Under
`Current hypothesis and rival`, descriptive or predictive work may state its
estimand or prediction target and main validity threat. A genotype comparison
does not require a rescue or second perturbation merely to populate this section.
Preserve the biological purpose without forcing every question into mutually
exclusive mechanisms or assuming that mixed explanations cannot coexist.

## Refresh proportionately and state the limits

Reuse the last valid review for unchanged premises. A new material result, a
changed comparison, a newly found close paper, or an experiment-readiness review
triggers an update. Search the interval since the previous scan and broaden only
where the biological contrast requires it. Do not repeat an all-portfolio search
on every session or schedule monitoring without the owner's request.

Stop a bounded pass when the closest inspected experiment and leading rival can
be compared to the proposed tuple, or record the specific access/specification
gap that prevents that comparison. Save uninspected leads separately from read
evidence. Reviewers can challenge scope or interpretation; the authoring agent
does not self-certify novelty, completeness or laboratory feasibility.

The research gate checks registered question-context coverage, required sections,
local entrypoints and exact illustrative SVG hashes. It also detects a changed
working hypothesis that has not been reconciled with its guide. Those mechanical
checks do not verify that a search was performed or that a source supports a
claim. Source interpretation and governance changes require explicit review;
protected integration remains an external setting, not a property of prose.
