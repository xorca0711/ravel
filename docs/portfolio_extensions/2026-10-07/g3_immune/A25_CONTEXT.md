# A25 region and sex context extension

## Published starting point

[Li et al., Nature Aging 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00479-x), Results “Characteristics of age-dependent genes” and Figure 1b–e, report sex-dependent age patterns. Later single-cell work focused on females. This constrains interpreting a mixed-sex atlas whose oldest mice are all male.

[Shea and Villeda et al., eLife 97671, 2025](https://elifesciences.org/articles/97671), Discussion paragraphs on regional specificity and intermediate states, explain why region averaging may obscure hippocampal patterns. Their state/function experiments are primary precedents; this atlas occupancy extension does not repeat them.

## Current hypothesis and rival

Estimand: equal-mouse age differences in source cluster fractions within each of cortex, cerebellum, hippocampus and striatum; plus pooled versus equally weighted regions among the same complete-region mice. The validity threats are regional sampling, sex/age support, processing and cluster construction on exposed data. The primary biological unit is mouse, with region/cells nested. All-sex contrasts are descriptive age/sex mixtures; male-only contrasts remain available where qualified; female old-age contrasts must remain held.

## What the comparison would teach

Differences between pooled and equal-region estimates reveal sensitivity to regional capture among complete mice. Persistence across regions limits this specific mixture explanation without establishing a common age mechanism. Missing complete mice or unstable mouse-deletion ranges restrict the comparison. Fractions describe captured cells, not absolute abundance. A distinct-state versus continuum/endpoint-mixture decision still requires training-only fitting and held-out mice under a separate contract.
