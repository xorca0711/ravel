# Candidate from Nb4-P09: adult fibroblast GRIA1-dependent acute response

**Recommendation to the integrating agent: justified as a proposed question,
with function and laboratory readiness unresolved. No global ID is allocated
here.** The scope develops the existing article-local Nb4-P09; it does not
claim a new sensory cell type, first discovery or an accepted mechanism.

## Question and evidence chain

**Does GRIA1 contribute to an acute glutamate-evoked response in independently
identified adult alveolar fibroblasts?**

Working hypothesis: adult alveolar fibroblasts with independently verified
identity and qualified receptor availability have a measurable acute
glutamate-evoked response that is reduced by a valid GRIA1 perturbation
relative to its matched control. The competing account is that RNA marks
identity or latent availability without functional receptor competence; a
response could instead reflect other receptors, altered health/state or
contaminating neural cells. SCN7A is contextual expression evidence, not
an assumed excitability mechanism or mandatory inclusion criterion.

The chain is explicit: published lung fibroblast receptor expression →
repository separate-gene transport and assay-sensitive co-detection →
unmeasured receptor function → matched GRIA1 perturbation/control acute
response → learn whether the expression premise has a functional consequence.
[Measured evidence](RESULTS.md) and [primary-literature/access record](LITERATURE.md)
are separate authorities.

The older [Valenzi study](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2018-214865),
Fig.5B/E, already identifies GRIA1-rich SPINT2 fibroblasts. Travaglini's
atlas predicts a related function from expression. Madissoon and Hall make
neural SCN7A a real identity rival. The cardiac glutamate study tests another
organ and principally GRIA3/GRIN2 context; the developmental and adrenergic
lung nerve studies prevent a generic nerve–fibroblast novelty claim. The
contribution is a **specific context/endpoint extension**, subject to the
bounded search and source-access limits, not discovery from missing hits.

## Conventional wet baseline and estimand

Use independently sourced adult alveolar-fibroblast preparations, with identity
and lack of neural contamination established independently of SCN7A/GRIA1.
Preserve the same starting state, culture history and challenge context in
matched **GRIA1 perturbation and control** preparations. Donor or independently
derived animal/preparation is the biological unit; wells/cells are nested.
Randomization and blinded measurement within the feasible preparation are
prospective design choices, not facts established by the atlas.

Primary candidate endpoint: a calibrated acute glutamate-evoked cellular
response, measured as challenge-minus-vehicle change and compared between
perturbation and control. Direct electrophysiological current is one
fit-for-purpose candidate; a validated calcium response could instead serve
as a downstream response endpoint, with its broader receptor specificity
explicit. Select and validate **one primary readout** before collecting
comparative outcomes; no assay has been chosen as ready here. Response
amplitude, kinetics and viability should not be conflated with proliferation,
matrix deposition or epithelial support.

The conventional baseline comparison can estimate a GRIA1 perturbation effect
when identity, perturbation validity, assay performance and biological units
are qualified. A rescue, factorial receptor panel, neuronal co-culture or
repair assay is not automatically required. Add an orthogonal test only if
off-target effects, receptor-complex specificity or a stronger mechanistic
claim makes it necessary. SCN7A should not define a favorable post-treatment
subset. Receptor protein/availability and viable cell/state retention are
separate qualification measurements, not automatic post-treatment adjustments
of the primary total effect.

## Informative outcomes and unresolved inputs

| Result under a qualified assay and perturbation | What it permits |
|---|---|
| Control response with a reduction after GRIA1 perturbation | Supports GRIA1 contribution to that acute response in that preparation; not direct binding, sodium sensing or repair |
| Response persists despite qualified perturbation and adequate precision | Weakens GRIA1 dependence; other receptors or routes remain plausible |
| Neither arm responds but the assay/challenge positive control is valid | Narrows the expression-to-function premise in that tested context; culture-state loss remains a concern |
| Perturbation changes survival, identity or assay detectability | Acute-response interpretation is ambiguous until those differences are resolved |
| No receptor localization, poor engagement, too few units or insensitive assay | Hold, not evidence of no function |

Actual host access, preparation feasibility, specific perturbation, stimulus
conditions, observation timing, primary assay calibration, positive control,
effect margin and unit-level variance/sample-size basis are unknown. No
numerical dose, sample size, power or equivalence margin is fabricated.
The next concrete decision is whether an accessible preparation preserves the
independently validated adult phenotype and permits a measurable acute assay.
