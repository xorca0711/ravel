## Reuse that is appropriate

Consistent typography, colour semantics, generic organelles and reuse of an identified cell class are appropriate. Different-looking art alone is not a quality criterion. The problem is a shared drawing that silently supplies a subtype, membrane topology, mechanism or result that the question does not establish.

- A1/A8/A10 legitimately share early-to-later comparison grammar; their predictors and later endpoints must remain different.
- A0/A5/A11 need two meaningful contexts, rather than a visually diminished reference-only control. A4/A17 need lineage/model comparisons. These are not the equal injury/control panels rejected by the owner.
- A3/A6 may share macrophage morphology; that does not transfer an ARG1 or STAT6 mechanism between questions.
- A18/A19/A20/A21/A32 may branch to separate readouts because the distinctions define those questions. Their arrows must not become causal mediation chains.
- A26/A27/A30 may share T-cell symbols; clone labels, state colours and paired compartments are illustrative, not measured repertoire frequencies or migration.
- A21's flattened endothelial silhouette and A25's ramified microglial icon can share primitive drawing code with other cells. Their labels/context must make identity clear; morphology is not a measured activation, ageing or differentiation result.
