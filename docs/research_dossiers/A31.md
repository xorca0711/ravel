## Biological problem

An RNA-defined lung fibroblast population can express neurotransmitter-receptor
genes without having a corresponding acute functional response. A31 asks whether
GRIA1 contributes to glutamate responsiveness in adult alveolar fibroblasts. It
does not presume that these cells are neurons, electrically excitable, a newly
discovered sensory population, or mediators of epithelial repair.

## Established knowledge

Lung atlases already describe GRIA1-enriched fibroblast populations. The
[bounded primary-source review](../portfolio_extensions/2026-10-07/g2_niche/LITERATURE.md)
also identifies neural SCN7A expression and functional nerve/fibroblast and
glutamate-response precedents in other contexts. Those precedents restrict broad
novelty claims. The proposed increment is a directly measured, GRIA1-dependent
acute response in a qualified adult alveolar-fibroblast preparation, rather than
another RNA label or a generic nerve–fibroblast interaction claim.

## Repository evidence

The new [Nb4-P09 run](../../analysis/research/runs/nb4_p09_expression_specificity_v1_20261007/receipt.json)
uses fixed genes, full-library denominators and donor/assay/region qualification.
GRIA1 and SCN7A are each higher in every eligible matched alveolar-versus-adventitial
donor comparison. Source 10x and SS2 share two qualifying donors; external cells
have four qualifying donors and nuclei two, including one shared donor. These
comprise two source donors and five external donors across repeated assays. Joint detection varies with assay, depth and
region. The source populations/labels are exposed; ambient RNA and label
independence remain unresolved. See the [measured report](../portfolio_extensions/2026-10-07/g2_niche/RESULTS.md).

## Unresolved gap

No current repository measurement establishes membrane-localized GRIA1, acute
glutamate response, target engagement or a response change after GRIA1
perturbation in adult alveolar fibroblasts. RNA co-detection does not bridge
those gaps. Functional assay choice, donor availability, response variability,
meaningful effect margin and sample-size justification remain unknown.

## Working hypothesis

Independently identified adult human alveolar fibroblasts exhibit an acute glutamate-evoked response with a GRIA1-dependent component: qualified GRIA1 perturbation reduces the challenge-minus-vehicle response relative to matched control preparations under the same exposure.

## Competing explanations

GRIA1 RNA may lack functional surface receptor or may reflect state/annotation
selection, ambient RNA or mixed neural cells. A glutamate response could instead
arise through other receptors or transport/metabolic effects. A perturbation
effect could reflect impaired viability or altered cell identity. These
explanations may coexist; the baseline need not identify every molecular route.
SCN7A co-detection is supporting expression context, not an inclusion gate or a
necessary mediator in this question.

## Discriminating outcomes

A reproducible acute response that changes with verified GRIA1 perturbation,
with viability and identity comparable, supports a contribution in that model.
A response without a qualified perturbation effect leaves GRIA1 dependence
unsupported within the achieved precision. Absence of response in a calibrated
assay limits functional interpretation of RNA expression in that preparation.
An effect accompanied by identity loss or reduced viable cells is ambiguous.
Neither a null result nor a wide interval establishes absence in every context.

## Next investigation

Read the [development plan](../../RQ_Specified/A31_gria1_fibroblast_response/PLAN.md).
First qualify independent fibroblast identity, adult anatomical provenance,
functional receptor localization/engagement and an acute response assay. Select
one primary endpoint and timing from that qualification. A new public dataset
is useful only if it links these measurements to perturbation/control and
independent preparations; another unlinked atlas cannot complete the test.
Any new numerical fit needs its own frozen contract and honest exposure record.

## Experimental bridge

Use donor-matched control and GRIA1-perturbed adult alveolar-fibroblast
preparations under the same qualified glutamate exposure. Donor is the primary biological unit; repeated isolations, cultures, preparations,
wells and cells are nested within donor. Define the acute response as challenge
minus matched vehicle, then compare that response between perturbation and
control. Record baseline, target engagement, viable cell amount and identity.
A receptor-proximal current or other calibrated readout must be chosen and
validated before claiming receptor activity; calcium alone is not automatically
GRIA1-specific. The vehicle measurement defines the evoked-response estimand rather than a
compulsory mechanistic rival arm. Viability and identity are qualification and
outcome measurements, not automatic adjustment variables or survivor-selection
gates after treatment. A rescue
or other-receptor arm is a later specificity option when justified, not a
compulsory rival arm. No dose, model, effect threshold or replicate number is
invented here.

## Interpretation boundary

The existing result is RNA expression transport, not the proposed functional
test. A qualified perturbation effect would apply to the chosen preparation,
exposure and measured endpoint; it would not establish neuronal excitability,
SCN7A sodium sensing, a lung-wide sensory system or improved alveolar repair.
Repair support would require a separately justified epithelial functional test.
