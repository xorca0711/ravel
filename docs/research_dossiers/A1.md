Do regulatory programmes distinguish RNA-similar transitional states and their functional responses?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Does regulatory state distinguish transition-associated epithelial populations with different subsequent capacities for mature recovery?

## Established knowledge

Transitional RNA resemblance can coexist with regulatory differences. Separate chromatin, methylation and lineage measurements answer different questions unless the source design links them.

## Repository evidence

The workspace compares regulatory and RNA evidence, including histone and pathway sensitivity checks. Its comparison matrix does not establish paired early regulatory state and later fate in the same biological units.

## Unresolved gap

Which regulatory feature precedes a difference in independently measured mature output, rather than merely distinguishing contemporaneous states, is unresolved.

## Working hypothesis

RNA-similar transitional alveolar epithelial cells may differ in regulatory competence for later AT1 maturation. The remaining hypothesis would require an early regulatory feature to distinguish later lineage-derived mature AT1 output beyond the starting RNA state. No particular regulatory feature has yet been nominated for that prospective contrast; a generic chromatin assay does not complete it.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Feature nomination required. Regulatory heterogeneity and functional transitional states are published. Exact-feature novelty cannot be judged until the regulatory feature and mature endpoint are fixed. See the [source comparison](NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a1). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Regulatory differences reflect composition, collection time or assay processing; apparent competence is inferred from markers already used to label the population.

## Discriminating outcomes

Early regulatory information that improves prediction in independent biological units would support a competence marker. It would not establish a causal regulator; absent incremental information weakens the proposed marker. Current unlinked RNA/regulatory samples cannot perform this comparison.

## Next investigation

Audit prospective linkage and sample identities before selecting a feature. Use existing comparisons to nominate a bounded feature; do not call unpaired assay agreement validation.

## Experimental bridge

A lineage-compatible system would compare early feature-defined populations and later mature output with matched viability and starting composition. Perturbation would be a separate causal step after prediction is established.

## Interpretation boundary

Accessibility, histone marks and RNA similarity do not by themselves demonstrate recoverability or irreversible commitment.

## Decision and maturity

Measurement groundwork exists; the outcome-linked design is missing. Keep this question available without presenting it as bench-ready.

Novelty remains provisional: review primary literature around the actual discriminator before calling this a new contribution. Assay validity, meaningful effect size, biological-unit variance and model feasibility must be established before a confirmatory experiment.

## Primary-source and feasibility review
