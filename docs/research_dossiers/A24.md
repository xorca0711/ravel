## Biological problem

Does aged bladder stroma contribute to loss of urothelial barrier maintenance beyond epithelial-intrinsic ageing and changing epithelial composition?

## Established knowledge

Differentiated umbrella cells can show senescence-like features across the lifespan (Al-Naggar et al., doi:10.1111/acel.14399); aged fibroblasts have been studied in bladder tumour permissiveness (Meguro et al., doi:10.1038/s43587-024-00704-1). Neither establishes this proposed normal-maintenance route. Broad urothelial ageing is also an established subject, not a new premise.

## Repository evidence

In three young and three old mice, the 24-minus-3-month bladder Cdkn2a detection difference is -1.238 percentage points. Lmnb1 detection decreases within the broad urothelial annotation (-1.519 points) but increases within mesenchymal cells (+3.952 points), despite an aggregate increase. These exposed source summaries measure detection frequency, distinct from normalized mean RNA, senescence burden or barrier function. All old mice are male; capture, sex and finer-state replacement within broad annotations remain limitations. No stromal mediator is selected.

## Unresolved gap

A specified normal-bladder stromal population, linked epithelial consequence and independent barrier measurement are not supplied by the atlas observations.

## Working hypothesis

An age-associated change in a specified bladder stromal population reduces urothelial barrier maintenance beyond epithelial age and terminal differentiation alone. This is a proposed intercompartmental contribution, not a conclusion from the atlas. The particular stromal population and mechanism remain open.

## Competing explanations

Cell mixture, capture/detection, epithelial-intrinsic change and general ageing context can produce marker differences without a causal stromal contribution to barrier loss.

## Discriminating outcomes

A specified stromal alteration associated with an independent epithelial/barrier consequence would advance the support question. A pattern explained by epithelial composition or detection would limit it. Opposing senescence-associated transcripts alone choose neither mechanism.

Concordant changes would motivate a bounded support question; effects accounted for by epithelial state or capture would restrict it. ## Experimental bridge

At the conceptual level, separate stromal context from epithelial age while measuring barrier integrity, cell composition and survival independently. Animal or independently prepared biological material is the unit; cells and imaging fields are nested. Exact assay, meaningful effect, variance, resources and sample size remain unknown.

## Interpretation boundary

Descriptive compartment context with an untested stromal-to-barrier proposal. The conflicting marker directions usefully prevent treating a generic senescence score as the biological answer; they do not select a barrier mechanism or refute senescence in the tissue.
