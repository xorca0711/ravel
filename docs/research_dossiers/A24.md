## Biological problem

Does the aged bladder stromal compartment contribute to loss of normal urothelial barrier maintenance independently of epithelial-intrinsic ageing and changing epithelial differentiation?

## Established knowledge

Differentiated umbrella cells can show senescence-like features across the lifespan (Al-Naggar et al., doi:10.1111/acel.14399); aged fibroblasts have been studied in bladder tumour permissiveness (Meguro et al., doi:10.1038/s43587-024-00704-1). Neither establishes this proposed normal-maintenance route. Broad urothelial ageing is also an established subject, not a new premise.

## Repository evidence

The exposed Nb5 bladder decomposition has opposing Lmnb1 detection directions in broad urothelial and mesenchymal annotations, while captured-bladder Cdkn2a detection declines. Those RNA summaries do not identify a causal stromal population, absolute abundance, senescence burden or barrier dysfunction. Existing Figures 7 and 8 are contextual evidence only.

## Unresolved gap

The stromal population, its relevant change and a paired independent epithelial barrier endpoint remain unqualified. A mediator is not required to pose the question, but the source population and functional measurement must be nominated before an identifiable causal experiment.

## Working hypothesis

An age-associated change in a specified bladder stromal population reduces urothelial barrier maintenance beyond epithelial age and terminal differentiation alone. This is a proposed intercompartmental contribution, not a conclusion from the atlas. The particular stromal population and mechanism remain open.

## Competing explanations

Epithelial-intrinsic ageing or altered umbrella-cell differentiation explains the functional change, while stromal RNA and captured proportions merely covary with age. Differential survival, tissue sampling and immune or vascular changes can also explain an apparent stromal association.

## Discriminating outcomes

A separately measured barrier difference attributable to stromal context at comparable epithelial starting condition would support the stromal contribution. A difference confined to epithelial age or differentiation would favor the rival. Marker positivity, tissue-average RNA or a ligand-receptor score alone cannot discriminate them.

## Next investigation

Qualify normal-bladder stromal subpopulations and epithelial identities in source-independent material, then determine whether a valid barrier endpoint and independent animals are available. Keep cancer-selected fibroblasts and constitutive umbrella-cell phenotypes separate. Do not fit a senescence score as a substitute for the missing function.

## Experimental bridge

At the conceptual level, separate stromal context from epithelial age while measuring barrier integrity, cell composition and survival independently. Animal or independently prepared biological material is the unit; cells and imaging fields are nested. Exact assay, meaningful effect, variance, resources and sample size remain unknown.

## Interpretation boundary

RNA detection and captured fractions do not establish protein output, secretion, senescence, barrier failure or stromal causation. The normal bladder question is not inferred from the published tumour niche, and no therapeutic direction is assigned.
