Do persistent founder differences help explain unequal mutant clone growth?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Do persistent founder differences explain unequal mutant clone growth, and can observed clone sizes distinguish that account from reversible state switching?

## Established knowledge

Unequal clone sizes can arise from persistent founder heterogeneity or stochastic processes. A simulator defect does not refute source lineage observations.

## Repository evidence

The completed synthetic qualification uses terminal weeks 1/2/4 and the 11-mouse sampling design. Candidate size laws differ under uniform detection. Under unconstrained size/time-dependent detection, however, persistent-founder, switching and combined families can yield the same retained-size law. This is a mathematical existence result and a numerical diagnostic: no England clone outcome was fitted, and the constructed detection weights were not measured in the experiment.

Prior source outcomes and simulator/ascertainment results were exposed. The switching rates are illustrative numerical fixtures, not inferred biology or justified empirical search bounds. Synthetic replicates use the source sampling design; they add no independent mice.

[Current result, design and limits](../audits/2026-11-03-rq-evidence-trajectory/A17_TECHNICAL_RESULT.md). Existing reports retain their evidence authority; this synthesis runs no new analysis.

## Unresolved gap

The actual detection/merger process and a justified bounded model domain remain unqualified. Retained sizes alone do not identify founder incidence, extinction or longitudinal state transitions.

## Working hypothesis

Persistent differences inherited from founder lineages may contribute to unequal mutant clone growth beyond reversible state switching and observation effects. A fitted founder class is a latent model term, not a discovered cell type. The current comparison concerns detected clone-size distributions until ascertainment and longitudinal linkage are qualified; numerical solver and identifiability work remain unfinished.

## Competing explanations

Persistent founder differences and reversible switching remain biological alternatives. Selection of which clones are observed can mimic either. Uniform-detection distinguishability and unconstrained-observation ambiguity are different statements.

## Discriminating outcomes

If models remain observationally equivalent under independently justified detection and merger constraints, the retained-size endpoint cannot select among them. If those constraints make distinct predictions recoverable, a model-conditional comparison becomes informative; neither result independently identifies a founder cell type.

## Next investigation

Independent evidence constraining detection/merger and the model domain could justify a conditional prediction comparison. Distinguishable models under those constraints would make that comparison informative; continued ambiguity would retain the limit. Neither outcome makes a latent model class a molecular cell type.
