Does IPF alter shared macrophage states beyond changing their abundance?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Which IPF macrophage RNA differences reflect changing state composition, and which persist within comparably defined states?

## Established knowledge

Disease can change both state abundance and expression within states. These effects require separate estimands and adequate donor representation.

## Repository evidence

The available proliferating-state validation contains three IPF and one control donor. In the broader 254-candidate RNA gene-set screen, no candidate passes corrected discovery and validation under estimated intergene correlation; the post-primary fixed-0.01 sensitivity supports 30, including 28 macrophage sets. This sensitivity cannot replace the primary result or establish pathway activity. Different subtype labels and sparse support prevent a composition-only conclusion. Additional cells, gene sets or reuse through an atlas integration do not create independent control donors, and a more complex score does not supply a reproducible within-state target.

[Statistical correction and subtype support](../../analysis/corrections/statistics/README.md). Existing reports retain their evidence authority; this synthesis runs no new analysis.

## Unresolved gap

Donor/state support for a fixed within-state comparison and a specific biological consequence remain unqualified. Existing owner reservations remain in force; no retain/reject decision is created here.

## Working hypothesis

IPF may change macrophage activity within a shared macrophage state as well as change the abundance of that state. The current task is a donor-level within-state comparison; a specific effector and tissue consequence are not yet nominated. The available three-IPF/one-control comparison cannot establish a population-wide disease mechanism.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Effector/consequence and donor replication required. IPF macrophage states and mixture changes are published. A named, reproducible within-state effector/consequence would be needed for a mechanistic increment. See the [source comparison](NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a6). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Composition, annotation, donor variation, cell-cycle structure and correlated genes can alter a disease-associated summary. Finite-source descriptions and population-level disease effects require different levels of support.

## Discriminating outcomes

A reproducible within-state contrast across independently supported donors would motivate a specific effector/consequence question. A pattern depending on state mixture or one donor would remain source-limited. Sparse support cannot establish a population null.

## Next investigation

Use the result and rationale assessment to decide whether another comparison is justified. Reuse the current decomposition and correlation checks. A new source would need adequate donor/state support for a stated contrast, rather than another broad signature. A reproducible within-state pattern would motivate a more specific consequence question; dependence on composition or a single donor would restrict the claim. No new target is selected by the current dataset.

## Experimental bridge

A later functional comparison would test the nominated macrophage output against a defined recipient response, with abundance and viability measured independently.

## Interpretation boundary

A coherent question with sparse source support and a useful interpretation limit. The current evidence has not selected a reproducible IPF effector; it also does not establish absence of within-state biology or demonstrate that the observed signal is random noise.
