Do fibroblast programmes add information about epithelial plasticity beyond macrophage IL1B?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

What fibroblast contribution, if any, adds explanatory information about epithelial context beyond the measured macrophage IL1B term?

## Established knowledge

Fibroblast inflammatory, matrix and trophic functions differ. Published niche studies already support mesenchymal contributions to epithelial repair and reciprocal epithelial–fibroblast effects. A specific source output and its added explanatory value are needed.

## Repository evidence

The exposed 12-patient paired-triad extension adds the fibroblast TGF-hallmark RNA proxy to macrophage IL1B, mixture and TNF predictors of the epithelial HPCS RNA proxy. Primary held-out MSE worsens by 0.004181 despite smaller individual errors in eight patients; every eligible prespecified sensitivity also worsens. This is an unfavorable predictive result and selects no mediator. Source capture and cohort coverage constrain the replication question. The failed predictor is a specific operational test rather than a comprehensive assay of fibroblast biology. The compartment rule was explicitly amended from the historical gate, and the specification followed prior outcome and A12-result exposure; it is not independent evidence or an execution of the old test.

## Unresolved gap

A specific source product and an independently measured epithelial consequence remain unnominated. The assigned macrophage term does not establish all active IL1B input or exhaust other cell sources.

## Working hypothesis

Fibroblast output may alter epithelial recovery beyond the effect of macrophage IL-1-beta input. The existing TGF-beta-hallmark/HPCS-proxy pilot found no aggregate held-out gain and nominates no mediator. The biological idea remains incomplete until a particular fibroblast product, epithelial response and source-directed contrast are supported; output nomination is preparatory work, not the hypothesis itself.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Biological mediator and endpoint required. Reciprocal epithelial-fibroblast effects and inflammatory fibroblast outputs already have functional precedents. A generic additional fibroblast effect or another score fit would not establish novelty. See the [source comparison](NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a13). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Source capture, composition, an unsuitable proxy or genuinely limited incremental information can explain the failure. None selects a particular fibroblast mediator or a feedback mechanism.

## Discriminating outcomes

A source-product comparison adding information about an independently measured epithelial consequence would advance that specified question. Repeating failure of generic pathway proxies would not identify a mediator or exclude all stromal contributions. Reciprocal feedback needs its own evidence.

## Next investigation

Use the result and rationale assessment to decide whether another comparison is justified. Retain the unfavorable operational result. A future extension should follow a specific published or measured source-product premise and a consequence that could distinguish it. Evidence beyond the current proxy could advance that bounded question; another association in the same score family would not by itself identify feedback.

## Experimental bridge

A future source-directed comparison needs fibroblast viability and output measurements plus a separately measured epithelial recovery endpoint and interpretable inflammatory input. A13 concerns fibroblast-to-epithelium contribution; A22 retains epithelial-to-fibroblast chemokine regulation.

## Interpretation boundary

Useful unfavorable prediction result. It restricts this proxy extension and does not establish absence of all fibroblast contributions. Replacing it with another generic pathway score would require a new biological rationale, not merely an opportunity to obtain a positive result.
