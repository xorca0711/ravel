Does prior injury leave a macrophage programme that differs from normal aging?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Does prior injury leave a persistent macrophage programme after age and current tissue context are accounted for?

## Established knowledge

Age, tissue processing and macrophage composition can change measured programmes. Persistence after injury must be distinguished from ongoing environmental stimulation.

## Repository evidence

W1 is a pseudobulk analysis, not a completed Compass flux analysis. Age and processing are confounded; the reference CAMERA analysis found no significant sets and the seven-gene ornithine set was ineligible.

Evidence locators (source citations and numerical details remain in these records):

- [Research Article/gate1_01_niethamer_2025/README.md](../../Research%20Article/gate1_01_niethamer_2025/README.md)

## Unresolved gap

An age-matched, independently replicated comparison separating within-state changes from abundance and current environment is missing.

## Working hypothesis

A bounded macrophage programme remains associated with prior injury within comparable states after relevant environmental and age differences are addressed.

## Competing explanations

The signal is normal aging, processing variation, altered macrophage mixture or continuing tissue stimulation.

## Discriminating outcomes

A history-associated change surviving the matched comparison supports persistence. Disappearance with age, processing or state adjustment favors the corresponding rival.

## Next investigation

Recover source ages, processing batches and independent sample identities. Define eligibility before another pathway analysis; preserve the negative and ineligible findings.

## Experimental bridge

A later experiment would distinguish prior history from present environment using comparable macrophage populations and an independently measured function. Persistent RNA alone cannot establish intrinsic memory.

## Interpretation boundary

Neither a pathway score nor an ineligible metabolic gene set demonstrates flux or trained immunity.

## Decision and maturity

Design unresolved. Metadata recovery is useful now; another unconstrained gene-set search would not answer the question.

Novelty remains provisional: review primary literature around the actual discriminator before calling this a new contribution. Assay validity, meaningful effect size, biological-unit variance and model feasibility must be established before a confirmatory experiment.

## Primary-source and feasibility review

Persistent macrophage effects after prior injury are already reported. Current age/context and origin differences must be resolved before interpreting an intrinsic memory mechanism.
