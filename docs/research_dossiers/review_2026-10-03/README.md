## Shared interpretation rules

1. Define the biological quantity first: absolute mature descendants, source output, receptor activity and survival are different endpoints. A gene score cannot silently replace any of them.
2. Separate the total effect from a proposed route. Cell number, identity, survival and the early pool may change *because of* the intervention. Conditioning on these measurements can remove part of the effect or select noncomparable survivors. Report their trajectories; require a justified design and assumptions for mediation.
3. Use pre-intervention state definitions for starting-state questions. A label assigned after perturbation describes a conditional group unless stronger identification is established. Broad effects and state interactions can coexist; nonsignificance does not establish equivalence.
4. Recover preparations, animals, donors, pools and split wells before inference. More cells or technical wells do not create biological replication. Overlapping atlas contributors do not supply external validation.
5. Treat missing timing, functional measurement or linkage as a reason to hold the intended claim. Extra correlations cannot repair an absent endpoint. Preserve failed transfer and weak transport when revising the next question.
