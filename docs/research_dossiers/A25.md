## Biological problem

Does a region- and sex-qualified middle-age microglial RNA distribution require an additional component beyond endpoint mixtures or a flexible age response?

## Established knowledge

Li et al. 2023 (doi:10.1038/s43587-023-00479-x) already report sex-dependent microglial ageing and a female middle-age pattern. Hammond et al. 2019 establish lifespan-related microglial diversity. The organizing question is model discrimination and transport in defined contexts, not discovery of generic ageing states.

The wider topic search also located [Shea and Villeda, eLife 97671](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97671), which already addresses hippocampal intermediate ageing states and functional investigations. Region-specific intermediate-state discovery is therefore not the proposed increment. The internal male-cortex comparison is now complete with a mixed result; external animal-unit qualification remains unresolved. The older [access record](literature_context_2026-10-03/SOURCE_UPDATE.md) retains its review-date scope.

## Repository evidence

The exposed internal prediction comparison uses 2,129 male cortex microglia from nine mice: 3/2/4 mice at 3/18/24 months. Whole mice are held out; feature selection, scaling and PCA use training mice. The added component beats endpoint mixture in both middle-age holdouts, but its gains against the flexible nonmonotone age model are +0.209465 and -0.013622 nats per cell in each fold's projection. The unfavorable comparison remains part of the result.

The flexible baseline allows nonmonotone quadratic mean/log-variance interpolation and is saturated at the three observed ages. Away from middle age, the added-component model equals the endpoint mixture by design; an aggregate advantage over flexible age is therefore not wholly attributable to the middle-age component.

[Current result, design and limits](../audits/2026-11-03-rq-evidence-trajectory/A25_TECHNICAL_RESULT.md). Existing reports retain their evidence authority; this synthesis runs no new analysis.

## Unresolved gap

Only two middle-age male cortex units support the key comparison. Other regions and sexes are not supplied by adjustment. Gaussian restrictions and age-associated processing remain alternatives; a predictive component is not a functional or discrete cell state.

## Working hypothesis

A region- and sex-qualified middle-age microglial distribution contains coherent structure that generalizes across animals and is inadequately represented by endpoint mixtures or a smooth age-response baseline. This is an RNA-organization hypothesis; a discrete cell state or functional role requires additional evidence.

## Competing explanations

A continuous or nonlinear age response, shifting mixtures and processing can generate the apparent configuration. The flexible baseline was actually tested and must not be replaced in the narrative by a weak straight-line baseline.

## Discriminating outcomes

Consistent held-out gain beyond flexible age would support an additional context-specific RNA organization. Gain only beyond endpoint mixture would instead expose the weakness of that representation. The observed flexible-age gain is mixed, so no discrete-state conclusion is selected.

## Next investigation

Use the result and rationale assessment to decide whether another comparison is justified. Treat this comparison as complete with a mixed outcome. A transport question would need independently justified middle-age, region and sex support with explicit preprocessing/model choices. Consistent improvement beyond flexible age would strengthen a bounded RNA-organization claim; gain only beyond endpoint mixture would continue to restrict it. No rescue tuning or new state label follows.

## Experimental bridge

Independent region- and sex-resolved biological material would test a frozen transferable configuration. Direct state/function or longitudinal linkage would be a later question. Internal animal holdout is useful exposed exploration, not independent-study confirmation or a disease-mechanism experiment.

## Interpretation boundary

Supported limitation of the endpoint-mixture representation, with mixed evidence against flexible age. A favorable average and similar reconstructed directions do not establish a distinct middle-age state. The two middle-age fits share most endpoint training mice and each trains on only one middle-age mouse; agreement is not independent replication.
