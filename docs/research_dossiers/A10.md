Do epithelial programmes add information about measured organoid growth?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Can epithelial RNA features predict later organoid growth across biological preparations, and how much of the existing result is concurrent within-screen information?

## Established knowledge

Area growth, survival, cell number and mature alveolar function are different outcomes. Concurrent RNA may describe growth without forecasting it.

## Repository evidence

A six-score growth block improves a specified baseline in the existing screen, but the full model has negative absolute held-out R-squared on three of four plates. Day-14 RNA and imaging are concurrent; targets and plates are entangled and preparation identities remain unresolved. Plates are not automatically independent biological preparations. Follow-up comparisons were specified before their new fits, after the original screen results were known; reuse of these wells is not independent validation.

[Current result, design and limits](../../RQ_Specified/A10_organoid_growth_outcome/reports/FOLLOWUP_RESULTS.md). Existing reports retain their evidence authority; this synthesis runs no new analysis.

## Unresolved gap

Earlier features, later growth and qualified independent preparation units are not linked in the current evidence. A new split of the same wells cannot supply missing temporal order or biological identity.

## Working hypothesis

The existing epithelial programme measurements may forecast subsequent organoid area beyond initial area and cell-cycle information across independent preparations. This is a growth-prediction question supporting the broader epithelial-growth hypothesis. The current day-14 RNA/area association is concurrent; it neither establishes early forecasting nor identifies a causal programme or mature repair function.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Prediction question; early timing and units held. The source screen already connects perturbations, RNA and organoid growth. The possible contribution is transport of a fixed early predictor to independent preparations, not reproduction of that association. See the [source comparison](../history/reviews/NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a10). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Concurrent phenotype, target/plate shifts, processing and preparation differences can explain apparent predictive information without transport to a new preparation or earlier time.

## Discriminating outcomes

Accurate transport of a frozen model from earlier features to later growth in qualified preparations would support forecasting. A gain confined to concurrent within-screen measurements would restrict that use. Relative improvement over a weak baseline does not establish adequate absolute prediction.

## Next investigation

Use the result and rationale assessment to decide whether another comparison is justified. Treat the current predictive comparison as complete. A forecast question would require a justified earlier-to-later measurement link and preparation-level evaluation. Transporting a frozen model would support that bounded use; dependence on concurrent measurements or plate context would restrict it. Do not label a within-screen gain a regenerative mechanism.

## Experimental bridge

Compare independent preparations with early measurements and later growth, recording survival and composition separately. A later perturbation-effect study needs a specified perturbation/control comparison and outcome; an effect comparison need not first establish a mechanism, and the RNA score itself is not one.

## Interpretation boundary

Within-screen information with a useful transport/timing limit. Relative model improvement does not establish reliable absolute prediction or an early biological driver. The poor plate results remain visible rather than being replaced by a favorable pooled association.
