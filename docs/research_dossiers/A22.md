Does epithelial identity determine fibroblast chemokine competence beyond epithelial amount?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

How far does the focal NKX2-1 epithelial identity result generalize, and does its fibroblast chemokine RNA effect extend to extracellular output?

## Established knowledge

Nabhan 2026 reports the focal NKX2-1 perturbation and fibroblast chemokine-RNA relationship. That premise does not establish a general identity rule across perturbations or extracellular protein output; the extension must specify which additional boundary or endpoint it addresses.

## Repository evidence

The retrospective predictor uses 672 wells across 201 targets. Adding the inherited AT2 identity score reduces equal-target mean squared error by 0.64% in target holdouts, increases it by 35.00% in whole-plate holdouts, and loses the small target-held-out gain without NKX2-1. Day-14 RNA and imaging are concurrent; day-7 imaging is also post-perturbation. Whole-screen TMM normalization was not refitted within folds, so held-out count distributions contributed to upstream processing. Adjustment for these treatment-dependent proxies does not identify mediation or an epithelial-amount effect. These are processing-conditional screen diagnostics, not independent preparation-level or clean prospective validation.

[Current result, design and limits](../../RQ_Specified/A22_epithelial_identity_niche_response/reports/identity_amount_v2/RESULTS.md). Existing reports retain their evidence authority; this synthesis runs no new analysis.

## Unresolved gap

Viable epithelial amount, extracellular chemokine protein and recipient selection/state are not jointly measured. Imaging area/read depth are imperfect proxies and may be treatment consequences.

## Working hypothesis

Loss of NKX2-1-dependent AT2 identity may lower actual fibroblast chemokine output within comparable fibroblast states beyond a change in viable epithelial source amount. The RNA direction is already published. The proposed extension asks whether it reaches extracellular protein output; the primary chemokine protein and collection/recovery definition have not been nominated, and broad AT2-score generalization failed.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Published focal effect; protein endpoint required. Nabhan already reports NKX2-1-dependent fibroblast chemokine transcripts and niche changes. The possible contribution is source-amount/selection-qualified protein output, not rediscovery of the transcript effect or an assumed immune-recruitment function. See the [source comparison](NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a22). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Source amount, fibroblast selection/survival and target-specific effects can explain the focal pattern without a general identity mechanism. Failed transfer does not establish an amount mechanism or absence of any identity-related biology.

## Discriminating outcomes

Extracellular protein changes alongside focal RNA differences, measured in qualified source/recipient contexts, would advance the output question. RNA-only changes or changes explained by recipient selection would limit that inference. Neither outcome establishes a general identity rule across targets.

## Next investigation

Use the result and rationale assessment to decide whether another comparison is justified. Retain the failed broad predictor. A focal extension would specify an extracellular output and how source amount and recipient context affect its interpretation. Concordant RNA/protein changes in qualified units would advance the output question; RNA-only or selection-dependent effects would restrict it. Another general predictor or RNA score does not supply secretion.

## Experimental bridge

A future comparison should jointly measure epithelial amount, fibroblast starting state and survival, and actual chemokine output; distinguish induction from selection over time.

## Interpretation boundary

Useful failed transport of the broad identity predictor. A focal NKX2-1 output question remains justified by the published contrast; it does not rescue the failed generalization or identify a new mediator. The seven focal chemokine RNA effects are not measurements of secretion or functional competence.
