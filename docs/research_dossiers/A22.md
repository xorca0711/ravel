Does epithelial identity determine fibroblast chemokine competence beyond epithelial amount?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Does the published fibroblast chemokine RNA reduction after epithelial NKX2-1 loss extend to actual extracellular protein output within comparable recipient states and viable source amount?

## Established knowledge

Nabhan 2026 already establishes the central epithelial-identity/niche relationship. A useful extension must distinguish a remaining mechanism or boundary rather than restate the published result.

## Repository evidence

The 672-well, 201-target analysis showed a 0.64% error improvement that became -0.97% without NKX21 and -35% on whole-plate holdout. This weakens a general predictor. Nb4 shows opposing C3/chemokine behavior across cohorts, so C3 is not a stable proxy.

## Unresolved gap

Within-state chemokine induction has not been separated from fibroblast abundance/selection, epithelial amount and a dominant perturbation-specific effect.

## Working hypothesis

Loss of NKX2-1-dependent AT2 identity may lower actual fibroblast chemokine output within comparable fibroblast states beyond a change in viable epithelial source amount. The RNA direction is already published. The proposed extension asks whether it reaches extracellular protein output; the primary chemokine protein and collection/recovery definition have not been nominated, and broad AT2-score generalization failed.

**Specificity/novelty disposition:** Published focal effect; protein endpoint required. Nabhan already reports NKX2-1-dependent fibroblast chemokine transcripts and niche changes. The possible contribution is source-amount/selection-qualified protein output, not rediscovery of the transcript effect or an assumed immune-recruitment function. See the [source comparison](NOVELTY_SPECIFICITY_APPLICATION_2026-10-03.md#a22). This is a proposed research scope, not a claim-grade or retain/reject decision.

## Competing explanations

Fibroblast selection, survival, epithelial amount or a target-specific effect unrelated to the proposed alveolar-identity route explains the output. The focal NKX2-1 contrast does not establish generality across epithelial perturbations.

## Discriminating outcomes

Lower measured protein output within comparable recipient states at comparable viable epithelial input would support the extension. An amount/selection-only effect or unchanged protein weakens it. Calibrated collection/recovery is needed for secretion-rate language; the failed broad predictor cannot support all epithelial identity perturbations.

## Next investigation

Retain the poor transport result and use a direct chemokine endpoint. Qualify fibroblast-state and source-unit measurements before fitting another general identity predictor.

## Experimental bridge

A future comparison should jointly measure epithelial amount, fibroblast starting state and survival, and actual chemokine output; distinguish induction from selection over time.

## Interpretation boundary

A score or C3 proxy does not establish secretion, recruitment or generalized epithelial identity control.

## Decision and maturity

The published relationship is established elsewhere; the proposed induction-versus-selection extension remains untested and novelty requires targeted review.

Novelty remains provisional: review primary literature around the actual discriminator before calling this a new contribution. Assay validity, meaningful effect size, biological-unit variance and model feasibility must be established before a confirmatory experiment.
