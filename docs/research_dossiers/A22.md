# A22: research development dossier

Does epithelial identity determine fibroblast chemokine competence beyond epithelial amount?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Does epithelial identity alter fibroblast chemokine output through within-state induction rather than fibroblast selection or epithelial amount?

## Established knowledge

Nabhan 2026 already establishes the central epithelial-identity/niche relationship. A useful extension must distinguish a remaining mechanism or boundary rather than restate the published result.

## Repository evidence

The 672-well, 201-target analysis showed a 0.64% error improvement that became -0.97% without NKX21 and -35% on whole-plate holdout. This weakens a general predictor. Nb4 shows opposing C3/chemokine behavior across cohorts, so C3 is not a stable proxy.

## Unresolved gap

Within-state chemokine induction has not been separated from fibroblast abundance/selection, epithelial amount and a dominant perturbation-specific effect.

## Working hypothesis

A defined epithelial identity change induces chemokine competence within comparable fibroblast states beyond epithelial amount.

## Competing explanations

Fibroblast selection, survival, epithelial abundance or an NKX21-specific effect explains the relationship.

## Discriminating outcomes

Within-state chemokine output after composition and epithelial amount are accounted for favors induction; an abundance-only or target-specific relationship favors the rivals.

## Next investigation

Retain the poor transport result and use a direct chemokine endpoint. Qualify fibroblast-state and source-unit measurements before fitting another general identity predictor.

## Experimental bridge

A future comparison should jointly measure epithelial amount, fibroblast starting state and survival, and actual chemokine output; distinguish induction from selection over time.

## Interpretation boundary

A score or C3 proxy does not establish secretion, recruitment or generalized epithelial identity control.

## Decision and maturity

The published relationship is established elsewhere; the proposed induction-versus-selection extension remains untested and novelty requires targeted review.

Novelty remains provisional: review primary literature around the actual discriminator before calling this a new contribution. Assay validity, meaningful effect size, biological-unit variance and model feasibility must be established before a confirmatory experiment.
