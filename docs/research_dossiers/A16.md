# A16: research development dossier

Does CD177 identify a priming phenotype within comparable mutant cells?

Status: proposed refinement, 3 October 2026. This is a development aid, not scientific acceptance or an experimental protocol.

## Biological problem

Does CD177 identify a reproducible phenotype within comparable mutant cells that predicts a later response beyond state composition?

## Established knowledge

A marker-associated mutant state may reflect current state, contamination or composition. Priming requires a subsequent response, not only a baseline association.

## Repository evidence

Corrected Stage 1 analysis attenuated the association and left residual imbalance. Current evidence does not establish intrinsic priming, complete exclusion of contamination or later fate.

## Unresolved gap

Protein-level attribution and a subsequent independently measured phenotype in matched mutant states are missing.

## Working hypothesis

CD177 identifies a within-state phenotype associated with a later defined response in mutant epithelium.

## Competing explanations

Expression marks another cell source, technical contamination, unequal starting states or an already active response.

## Discriminating outcomes

Reproducible epithelial protein attribution and a later response difference within comparable starting states favor the hypothesis; disappearance with state/source controls favors the rival.

## Next investigation

Use the corrected state-attribution report and explicitly track remaining imbalance. Qualify protein/source specificity and response timing before another priming analysis.

## Experimental bridge

A future matched-state comparison should separate baseline marker attribution, later response, survival and descendant fate across independent preparations.

## Interpretation boundary

A corrected association is not proof of cell-intrinsic priming, absence of contamination or a lineage advantage.

## Decision and maturity

State-attribution evidence is bounded. Priming remains a proposed interpretation requiring distinct measurements.

Novelty remains provisional: review primary literature around the actual discriminator before calling this a new contribution. Assay validity, meaningful effect size, biological-unit variance and model feasibility must be established before a confirmatory experiment.
