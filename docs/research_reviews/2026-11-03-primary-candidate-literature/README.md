## What most changes the biological framing

1. **A3/DY1-P02:** consider an IRF4-associated loss of PPARgamma identity alongside
   the established STAT6–EGR2–PPARG/RXRA premise. Type-2 input does not specify one
   universal macrophage programme; alveolar macrophages and NAMs are distinct.
2. **A5:** separate developmental features associated with alveolar maturation
   from features associated with airway diversion. A developmental score alone
   cannot choose between these outcomes.
3. **A12:** ERBB4–NRG1 provides a concrete epithelial identity-stability precedent.
   It is a possible adjacent extension, not evidence that ERBB4 mediates the
   existing IL-1-associated result.
4. **A32:** distinguish ARG1-positive macrophage participation in repair from a
   beneficial effect of ARG1 catalytic activity. Matrix reduction and mature
   epithelial recovery still need separate interpretation.
5. **A31 and Wg-P06:** the closest inspected functional/substrate papers were
   already in the repo. This search does not justify adding extra targets simply
   to make these two candidates appear newer.

## New-to-inspected-repo papers

### A3 / DY1-P02 — macrophage identity and regulatory output

**Verwaerde et al., _Innate type 2 lymphocytes trigger an inflammatory switch in
alveolar macrophages_. Immunity 59:60–78.e9 (2026); online 11 December 2025,
issue 13 January 2026.**
[Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41386231/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2025.11.015).

The primary abstract reports an ILC2/IL-13–IRF4 programme that suppresses
PPARgamma-dependent homeostatic identity in resident alveolar macrophages and
promotes inflammatory output. This supplies a directionally different regulatory
precedent from simply treating type-2 exposure as PPARgamma reinforcement.
**Screening implication:** compare evidence for preserved PPARgamma identity,
IRF4-associated identity loss and continued niche input before interpreting a
late RNA/accessibility pattern as memory. These explanations can coexist.
**Limit:** allergen-exposed alveolar macrophages are not Damani-Yokota NAMs;
the paper does not establish persistence after stimulus cessation in that system.
Access: indexed primary abstract and publication metadata; full Results/figure
audit remains unread because the publisher returned 403.

### A5 — developmental reuse versus a different epithelial destination

**Dong et al., _Hypoxia promotes airway differentiation in the human lung
epithelium_. Cell Stem Cell 32:1705–1722.e9 (2025); online 10 October,
issue 6 November 2025.**
[Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41075787/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2025.09.007).

Human fetal/adult epithelial organoid results implicate HIF activity and KLF4/5
in airway fate acquisition, including aberrant basal-like differentiation from
AT2 cells. **Screening implication:** HIF/KLF-associated diversion is a specific
alternative to interpreting developmental-feature reuse as alveolar recovery.
It does not replace A5's fixed Guo component or make KLF4/5 established drivers
of that component. Access/locator: primary abstract; full figure audit unread
(publisher 403; PMC browser challenge).

**Liu et al., _Alveolar stem cells transdifferentiate to drive bronchiolar
regeneration_. Nature, online 7 October 2026.**
[Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11127-w),
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42844469/).

Reports AT2 participation in bronchiolar repair, with SPP1/ITGB1-associated
migration and Notch/RBPJ-dependent airway specification. **Screening implication:**
an AT2-derived regenerative state need not be progressing toward AT1. Distinguish
destination and tissue need; airway differentiation is not inherently maladaptive.
Access: indexed publisher Discussion/Extended Data Fig. 10 model, primary metadata
and Crossmark; no complete figure/methods audit. Direct publisher open failed.

**Klinkhammer et al., _Epithelial-mesenchymal cell competition coordinates fate
transitions across tissue compartments during lung development and fibrosis_.
Nature Communications 16:10956, 21 November 2025.**
[Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66690-z).

The AF1 fibroblast niche and its YAP/TAZ–MYC/SNAIL context affect epithelial
bronchiolization and fibrosis. Fig. 6 reports more bronchiolization and collagen
after AF1 Myc loss; the next Results section addresses myofibroblast-to-AF1
reversion. **Screening implication:** a developmental programme can have a
different meaning by compartment and transcriptional partner. This is a
mesenchymal explanation to compare with an epithelial-intrinsic A5 account,
not permission to merge the two into one score. Access: publisher Abstract,
selected Results/Fig. 1–2 and Fig. 6–7 text; supplements not audited.

### A12 — a concrete recipient-state precedent

**Lin et al., _Activation of ERBB4 Pathway Inhibits Pathological
Transdifferentiation of Lung Epithelial Progenitors into CD66c+ Basal Cells in
Severe Lung Injury_. Advanced Science 13:e19151 (2026); online 7 April 2026.**
[Primary record and figure legends](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41944369/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202519151).

Human distal-lung organoids link NRG1/ERBB4 signaling to restriction of
secretory/AT2 conversion into CD66c+ basal cells. Read **Fig. 6** for RNA/ATAC
and receptor context, then **Fig. 7**, especially the receptor-dependent
comparison in 7e–g, for the functional distinction.
**Screening implication:** nominate recipient receptor/identity state as a
specific biological modifier rather than a generic inflammatory score.
**Limit:** this does not show IL-1–ERBB4 coupling or explain A12's current
association. Cell-state preservation is not automatically mature AT1 recovery.
Access: primary abstract and figure legends. PubMed explicitly notes a
**21 April 2026 correction to Fig. 3m**; the corrected record was used, but the
underlying image replacement and full methods were not audited.

### A32 — matrix, epithelial recovery and cell-specific metabolism

**Sun et al., _S100B triggers neuroendocrine macrophage networks to drive airway
regeneration in mice_. Nature Communications, first online 17 December 2025;
2026 volume 17, article 965.**
[Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67691-8),
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41408059/).

Reports S100B-associated ARG1-positive macrophage–neuroendocrine/club-cell repair
crosstalk. **Screening implication:** ARG1-positive myeloid states can accompany
epithelial repair, so their abundance alone cannot select an anti-fibrotic
intervention. The useful distinction is enzyme-dependent matrix effects versus
other repair-supporting macrophage functions.
**Limit:** the inspected evidence does not isolate ARG1 catalytic necessity,
measure mature alveolar recovery or resolve the joint A32 outcome. The authors
also acknowledge uncertainty about the macrophage signal/niche role and broad
PGP9.5 labeling. Access: indexed primary Results/Discussion and Fig. 7; no full
methods/supplement audit. Fig. 7's caption says CSRP3 whereas its body describes
S100B; retain this editorial inconsistency rather than silently repairing it.

**Pantuck et al., _Glutaminase regulates alveolar regeneration through
autophagy-YAP/TAZ in pulmonary fibrosis_. American Journal of Respiratory Cell
and Molecular Biology, ahead of print 11 August 2026.**
[Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581588/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajrcmb/aanag145).

The abstract reports that AT2-specific GLS1 loss impairs AT2-to-AT1 differentiation
and worsens fibrosis, with excessive autophagy and YAP/TAZ loss.
**Screening implication:** epithelial metabolic support is an explicit competing
consideration when selecting a metabolism-related fibrosis target. This supports
A32's joint-outcome logic, not an ARG1–GLS1 connection. Access/locator: primary
abstract, Measurements and main results; figures and supplements unread.

**Speth et al., _Longitudinal single-cell RNA-seq profiling of lung cell
phenotypes, signaling, and cross-talk during fibrosis resolution_. Scientific
Reports 16:36191 (2026); published 14 October, VOR dated 30 October 2026.**
[Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-75301-w).

Longitudinal mouse fibrosis/resolution RNA profiles nominate HGF/MET, TWEAK and
cAMP-related changes across epithelial, fibroblast and macrophage compartments.
**Fig. 5** distinguishes epithelial states; **Fig. 6** presents inferred
intercellular communication. **Screening implication:** these are candidate
resolution-associated comparators for an ARG1/matrix-centred account, not proven
upstream drivers. CellChat and enrichment are RNA-derived predictions, not
measured signaling flux. Access: publisher selected Results and Fig. 5–6 legends.
The April 2026 preprint is the same study and is not independent evidence.

## Recommended reading order

For immediate hypothesis sharpening: **Verwaerde → Dong → Lin → Sun**.
Then Liu for the distinction between adaptive airway repair and pathological
diversion, and Pantuck for epithelial metabolic consequences. Klinkhammer and
Speth provide cross-compartment and resolution context. A31 and Wg-P06 should
keep their current direct questions rather than acquire unrelated new axes.

If the owner adopts one of these extensions, update that question's context,
dossier and illustrated README together and preserve the prior hypothesis and
results. This review itself makes no such adoption or human retain/reject decision.
