# Amendment: screening as progressive biological hypothesis narrowing

3 November 2026, Asia/Seoul. Owner clarification during technical execution.
This amends the emphasis and unstarted work in [the earlier proposal](ANALYSIS_PLAN.md).
It preserves completed analyses, contracts, hypotheses, question identities and
unfavorable findings. It does not introduce a new repository-wide policy.

## Purpose and reason

The owner uses Cardoso's successive epithelial, stromal and immune hypotheses
as an example of screening: identify a changed population, nominate its relevant
output, identify the responding population, discriminate the resulting biological
change, and examine downstream or feedback consequences. The earlier follow-up
queue emphasized annotation completeness and measurement robustness. Those can
support a candidate decision, but do not themselves supply the requested
progression from an organizing question to a narrower biological explanation.

Each next analysis must therefore answer: **which biological link or candidate
could this result strengthen, weaken, distinguish or leave unresolved?** A
negative result can redirect a branch; a technically successful run need not
narrow it. Descriptive target nomination is allowed before functional proof.
Missing proof of an entire pathway does not forbid an informative partial screen.

## Sequence for each existing owner

1. **Locate the unexplained observation.** Name the population, state, model,
   timing and biological consequence of interest. Separate what the paper
   established from what this repository measured.
2. **Draw the smallest plausible explanation.** Identify the proposed cell,
   regulator, signal, recipient or state transition. Mark each relationship as
   a published premise, repository observation, candidate explanation or unknown.
   A marker change does not automatically identify the driver.
3. **Identify the nearest useful branch point.** Examples include changed
   population abundance versus within-state change; continuing input versus
   maintained competence; different ligand availability versus recipient context;
   differentiation entry versus delayed exit. Explanations may coexist.
4. **Choose an informative screen.** Define candidates, comparator, endpoint,
   biological unit and the result that would change the candidate set. Use
   existing expression, annotation, temporal, spatial or perturbation evidence
   according to what it actually measures. Add technical checks only where they
   could alter that biological decision.
5. **Record the narrowing.** Report supported context, inconsistent candidates,
   unresolved alternatives and unsupported comparisons. A later shortlist is
   exposed exploratory selection and needs a new version; it cannot silently
   replace a fixed earlier panel.
6. **Advance one justified step.** Examine downstream output, persistence or
   feedback when the preceding evidence makes that next question useful. A
   plausible branch may proceed with explicit uncertainty; no serial chain is
   considered proved merely because all its genes appear in one dataset.

Not every RQ concerns intercellular signaling. A5 concerns programme components;
A8 has a prospective-output question; A17 concerns competing growth models;
A27 concerns within-state repertoire concentration. Their biological progression
must fit those questions rather than inherit a compulsory ligand–receptor format.
